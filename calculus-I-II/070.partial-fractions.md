# Partial Fractions

## 🧠 Big Idea

Partial fractions turn one complicated rational function into a sum of simpler rational functions. This is useful because basic fractions like $\frac{1}{x-a}$ integrate into logarithms.

The method works after the denominator has been factored. If the numerator has degree greater than or equal to the denominator, divide first; partial fractions are for the proper rational part that remains.

So the big move is not integration first. It is rewriting the rational function into pieces whose antiderivatives are familiar.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}=\frac{A}{x-r_1}+\frac{B}{x-r_2}+\cdots
$$

This means a rational expression with numerator $P(x)$ and factored denominator $Q(x)$ can often be rewritten as smaller pieces. The numbers $r_1,r_2,\ldots$ are roots of the denominator, and $A,B,\ldots$ are constants chosen to make the equality true.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of partial fractions as undoing the process of combining fractions. When fractions are combined, denominators multiply and the result can look harder than the pieces that created it. Partial fractions separate those pieces again.

Once separated, each denominator tells you which basic integral pattern to use.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Evaluate

$$
\int \frac{1}{x^2-1}\,dx
$$

Factor the denominator: $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$. Write

$$
\frac{1}{x^2-1}=\frac{A}{x-1}+\frac{B}{x+1}
$$

Multiplying by $(x-1)(x+1)$ gives $1=A(x+1)+B(x-1)$. Solving gives $A=\frac12$ and $B=-\frac12$. Therefore

$$
\int \frac{1}{x^2-1}\,dx=\frac12\ln|x-1|-\frac12\ln|x+1|+C
$$

This means the difficult-looking rational integral becomes two logarithmic integrals.

## 🔑 Remember

Partial fractions break a rational function into simpler fractions you already know how to integrate.
