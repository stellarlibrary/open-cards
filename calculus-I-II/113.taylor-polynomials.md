# Taylor Polynomials

## 🧠 Big Idea

A Taylor polynomial is a finite polynomial that imitates a function near a chosen center. It matches the function's value and several derivatives at that center.

The more derivative information you include, the better the polynomial usually fits nearby. Taylor polynomials are local approximations: they are designed to work best close to the center, not necessarily far away.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
P_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k
$$

This means the degree $n$ Taylor polynomial for $f$ centered at $a$ is built from the values of $f$ and its first $n$ derivatives at $a$. The term $f^{(k)}(a)$ gives the $k$th derivative at the center, $k!$ scales it correctly, and $(x-a)^k$ measures distance from the center.

## 👀 Intuition

A Taylor polynomial is like making a custom polynomial copy of a function. The constant term matches the height. The linear term matches the slope. The quadratic term helps match bending. Higher terms add finer corrections.

Near the center, these matching conditions make the polynomial look and behave like the original function.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $f(x)=e^x$ centered at $0$, every derivative is $e^x$, so every derivative value at $0$ is $1$. The third-degree Taylor polynomial is

$$
P_3(x)=1+x+\frac{x^2}{2!}+\frac{x^3}{3!}
$$

This polynomial approximates $e^x$ near $0$ by matching its value, slope, and first few curvature patterns there.

## 🔗 Connection

Taylor polynomials are partial sums of Taylor series.

## 🔑 Remember

A Taylor polynomial is a finite local copy built from derivative data at one point.
