# Endpoint Testing

## 🧠 Big Idea

Endpoint testing decides what happens at the boundary values of a power series. After the radius of convergence gives an open interval, the endpoints still need individual tests.

This step matters because the ratio and root tests usually produce equality at endpoints, such as a limit of $1$. When that happens, those tests give no conclusion, so we substitute each endpoint into the original series.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
x=a-R \quad \text{and} \quad x=a+R
$$

These are the two endpoint values for a power series centered at $a$ with radius $R$. To test them, plug each value of $x$ into the series and study the resulting numerical series.

Common tools include the $p$-series test, geometric series test, alternating series test, comparison tests, and divergence test.

## 👀 Intuition

Endpoint testing is like checking the two fence posts after you know the fence length. Points inside the fence are handled together, but the posts themselves require separate inspection.

Small changes at endpoints can turn a power series into very different numerical series. One may alternate and converge, while the other may become harmonic and diverge.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x-3)^n}{n}
$$

The radius is $R=1$, so the endpoints are $x=2$ and $x=4$.

At $x=4$, the series becomes $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}$, which diverges. At $x=2$, it becomes

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}
$$

This alternating harmonic series converges. Therefore the full interval includes $2$ but not $4$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Do not test endpoints using only the ratio test again; it is usually inconclusive there.

## 🔑 Remember

Find the radius first, then plug in each endpoint and test the new series.
