# Radius of Convergence

## 🧠 Big Idea

The radius of convergence tells how far from the center a power series is guaranteed to converge. It gives a distance, not the full interval.

For a power series centered at $a$, convergence usually happens for all $x$ values inside some symmetric window around $a$. Outside that window, the series diverges. The endpoints need separate attention.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
|x-a|<R
$$

This means the series converges whenever the distance from $x$ to the center $a$ is less than the radius $R$. The number $R$ may be finite, $0$, or infinite.

A common way to find $R$ is the ratio test:

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n}\right|<1
$$

Here $u_n$ is the $n$th term of the power series. The inequality describes where the terms shrink fast enough for convergence.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture dropping a circular safety zone around the center on the number line. Inside the zone, the infinite polynomial behaves well. Outside it, the terms do not settle down properly.

The radius is like the range of a signal from a radio tower: strong near the center, unreliable beyond a certain distance.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{2^n}
$$

The terms look like $(x/2)^n$. This is a geometric series, so it converges when

$$
\left|\frac{x}{2}\right|<1
$$

This inequality means $|x|<2$, so the radius of convergence is $R=2$.

## ⚠️ Careful

The radius does not tell you whether the endpoints converge. It only describes the open interval.

## 🔑 Remember

The radius of convergence is the distance from the center to the edge of guaranteed convergence.
