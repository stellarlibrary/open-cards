# Logarithmic Functions

## 🧠 Big Idea

A logarithm answers an exponent question.

When you see $\log_b(x)$, you are asking: “What power of $b$ gives $x$?” Logarithmic functions are the inverses of exponential functions, so they undo repeated multiplication.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\log_b(x)=y \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad b^y=x
$$

This means the logarithm is the exponent $y$ needed to make the base $b$ produce the number $x$. Here $b>0$, $b\neq 1$, and $x>0$.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of an exponential function as building a number by multiplying by the same base again and again. The logarithm works backward and asks how many multiplication steps were needed.

Because exponentials with positive bases never produce zero or negative outputs, logarithms only accept positive inputs.

This domain restriction comes from the inverse relationship, not from a separate rule to memorize.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Consider

$$
\log_2(8)=3
$$

This statement means $3$ is the exponent needed on base $2$.

Indeed,

$$
2^3=8
$$

This confirms that multiplying by $2$ three times produces $8$.

Another way to say the same thing is that the logarithm reverses the exponential equation $2^y=8$ and solves for the exponent $y$.

## ⚠️ Careful

$\log_b(x)$ is defined only for $x>0$. The input to a logarithm cannot be zero or negative in real-valued calculus.

## 🔑 Remember

A logarithm tells you the exponent.
