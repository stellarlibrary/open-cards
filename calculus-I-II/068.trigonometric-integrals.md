# Trigonometric Integrals

## 🧠 Big Idea

Trigonometric integrals involve powers or products of trig functions such as $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $\tan x$, and $\sec x$. The main skill is rewriting the expression until a substitution or basic antiderivative appears.

These problems are not about memorizing every possible case. They are about using identities to expose a useful derivative pair, such as $\sin x$ with $\cos x$, or $\tan x$ with $\sec^2 x$.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sin^2 x+\cos^2 x=1, \qquad 1+\tan^2 x=\sec^2 x
$$

The first identity lets you trade between powers of sine and cosine. The second lets you trade between tangent and secant. These identities are useful because they can leave behind exactly the factor needed for substitution.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of trig identities as changing the language of the integrand. The amount of rotation has not changed, but the expression may become easier to read. Odd powers often let you save one factor for $du$ and convert the remaining even powers using an identity.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Evaluate

$$
\int \sin^3 x\cos^2 x\,dx
$$

Save one $\sin x$ and rewrite $\sin^2 x$ as $1-\cos^2 x$:

$$
\int (1-\cos^2 x)\cos^2 x\sin x\,dx
$$

This setup fits $u=\cos x$, so $du=-\sin x\,dx$. Then

$$
-\int (1-u^2)u^2\,du=-\frac{u^3}{3}+\frac{u^5}{5}+C
$$

So the answer is $\frac{\cos^5 x}{5}-\frac{\cos^3 x}{3}+C$.

## 🔑 Remember

Use trig identities to create a substitution, not just to make expressions look different.
