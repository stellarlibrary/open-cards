# Inflection Points

## 🧠 Big Idea

An inflection point is a point where the graph changes concavity. The graph switches from bending up to bending down, or from bending down to bending up.

Inflection points are about a change in shape, not just a special value of the second derivative.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f''(c)=0 \quad \text{or} \quad f''(c) \text{ does not exist}
$$

This equation gives possible locations for inflection points. The number $c$ is only a candidate. To be an actual inflection point, the concavity of $f$ must change as $x$ passes through $c$.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine tracing a curve with your finger. At first it bends like a cap, then gradually straightens, then bends like a cup. The transition point is where the graph changes the direction of its bend.

It is like a road changing from curving left to curving right. The direction of curvature, not just the height, is what matters.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^3$. Then

$$
f''(x)=6x
$$

The second derivative changes from negative to positive at $x=0$. Therefore the graph changes from concave down to concave up, so $(0,0)$ is an inflection point.

## ⚠️ Careful

The equation $f''(c)=0$ is not enough. For $f(x)=x^4$, $f''(0)=0$, but the graph is concave up on both sides of $0$, so there is no inflection point there.

## 🔑 Remember

An inflection point requires a real change in concavity.
