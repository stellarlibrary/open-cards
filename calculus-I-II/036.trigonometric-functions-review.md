# Trigonometric Functions Review

## 🧠 Big Idea

Trigonometric functions connect angles with coordinates and ratios.

In calculus, sine, cosine, and tangent are best understood through the unit circle. As an angle rotates, the point on the circle moves, and its coordinates create repeating wave patterns.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\cos\theta=x,\qquad \sin\theta=y,\qquad \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}
$$

This means that for an angle $\theta$ on the unit circle, cosine is the horizontal coordinate, sine is the vertical coordinate, and tangent is the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change when $x\neq 0$.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine a point traveling around a circle of radius $1$. Its shadow on the horizontal axis gives $\cos\theta$, and its height gives $\sin\theta$.

As the point keeps rotating, the same heights and horizontal positions repeat. This repeating behavior is why trigonometric functions are useful for waves, circular motion, and oscillation.

## 🔍 Simple Example

At angle $\theta=\frac{\pi}{6}$, the unit-circle point is

$$
\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
$$

This means the horizontal coordinate is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and the vertical coordinate is $\frac{1}{2}$.

Therefore,

$$
\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\qquad
\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)=\frac{1}{2}
$$

This gives the cosine and sine values directly from the point.

## ⚠️ Careful

Calculus formulas for trigonometric derivatives assume angles are measured in radians, not degrees.

## 🔗 Connection

Two facts follow from the unit circle. Because the radius is $1$, the Pythagorean identity $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ holds for every angle. And because the point returns to its start after a full turn, sine and cosine are **periodic** with period $2\pi$, which is what makes them model waves and oscillation.

## 🔑 Remember

On the unit circle, cosine is $x$ and sine is $y$.
