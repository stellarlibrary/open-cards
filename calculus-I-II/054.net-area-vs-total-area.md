# Net Area vs Total Area

## 🧠 Big Idea

Net area keeps track of signs. Area above the $x$-axis counts positive, and area below the $x$-axis counts negative.

Total area ignores signs. It measures how much area is covered altogether, whether the graph is above or below the axis.

These two ideas answer different questions: “What is the overall signed change?” versus “How much area was swept out?”

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\text{Net area}=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
$$

This means the definite integral adds positive contributions where $f(x)>0$ and negative contributions where $f(x)<0$.

$$
\text{Total area}=\int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx
$$

This means total area integrates the absolute value of $f$, so every contribution is counted as nonnegative area.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine walking forward and backward along a line. Net displacement cares about direction, so backward movement cancels forward movement. Total distance cares only how far you walked, so nothing cancels.

Net area is like displacement. Total area is like distance.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x$ on $[-1,1]$. The triangular region below the axis from $-1$ to $0$ has area $\frac{1}{2}$, and the triangular region above the axis from $0$ to $1$ also has area $\frac{1}{2}$.

The net area is $0$ because the signed pieces cancel. The total area is $1$ because both pieces count positively.

## ⚠️ Careful

Do not use a definite integral alone when a question asks for total geometric area and the graph crosses the axis.

## 🔑 Remember

Net area allows cancellation; total area counts every piece positively.
