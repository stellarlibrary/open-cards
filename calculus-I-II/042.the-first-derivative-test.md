# The First Derivative Test

## 🧠 Big Idea

The First Derivative Test uses the sign of $f'(x)$ around a critical number to decide whether the graph has a local maximum, a local minimum, or neither.

It works because $f'(x)$ tells whether the function is increasing or decreasing on each side.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f' \text{ changes } + \to - \Rightarrow \text{local maximum}, \qquad f' \text{ changes } - \to + \Rightarrow \text{local minimum}
$$

A change from positive to negative means the graph rises before $c$ and falls after $c$, creating a local high point. A change from negative to positive means the graph falls before $c$ and rises after $c$, creating a local low point.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of hiking over a hill. If your elevation is increasing before a point and decreasing after it, you reached a peak. If your elevation is decreasing before a point and increasing after it, you reached a valley.

If the sign of $f'$ does not change, you may simply flatten briefly and keep moving in the same overall direction.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^3-3x$. Then

$$
f'(x)=3(x-1)(x+1)
$$

The derivative is positive on $(-\infty,-1)$, negative on $(-1,1)$, and positive on $(1,\infty)$. So $x=-1$ gives a local maximum, and $x=1$ gives a local minimum.

## ⚠️ Careful

The test depends on signs on intervals around the critical number, not just the value of $f'(c)$ itself.

## 🔑 Remember

For the First Derivative Test, watch how $f'$ changes sign.
