# The Washer Method

## 🧠 Big Idea

The washer method finds volumes of solids of revolution that have a hole through the middle.

When a region is rotated around an axis, each thin slice may become a washer: a circular disk with a smaller circular disk removed. The volume comes from outer area minus inner area.

So the washer method is the disk method adjusted for empty space.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
V=\int_a^b \pi\big([R(x)]^2-[r(x)]^2\big)\,dx
$$

Here $V$ is volume, $[a,b]$ is the interval, $R(x)$ is the outer radius, and $r(x)$ is the inner radius. The expression $\pi [R(x)]^2$ is the full outer disk area, while $\pi [r(x)]^2$ is the missing inner disk area.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture stacking thin washers from a hardware store. Each washer has an outside edge and an inside hole.

A rotated region can create the same shape. At every position, the outer curve determines the outside radius, and the inner curve determines the hole radius.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Rotate the region between $y=2$ and $y=x$ from $x=0$ to $x=1$ around the $x$-axis.

The outer radius is $R(x)=2$, and the inner radius is $r(x)=x$. The volume is $\int_0^1 \pi(2^2-x^2)\,dx=\frac{11\pi}{3}$. This subtracts the empty inner space from the larger solid.

## ⚠️ Careful

Always square the radii before subtracting: use $R^2-r^2$, not $(R-r)^2$.

## 🔑 Remember

Washer method: outer disk minus inner disk.
