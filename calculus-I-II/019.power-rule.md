# Power Rule

## 🧠 Big Idea

The power rule is the fastest way to differentiate any power of $x$. Instead of using the limit definition every time, you just follow a simple pattern.

It's the first shortcut rule, and it handles polynomials, roots, and reciprocals all at once.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\,x^n=n\,x^{n-1}
$$

This means: **bring the exponent down** as a multiplier, then **subtract one** from the exponent. The rule works for any real exponent $n$ — positive, negative, or fractional.

## 👀 Intuition

A higher power makes a function steepen faster, so the exponent naturally shows up as the strength of the slope. Lowering the exponent by one reflects that a derivative describes change one "degree" simpler than the original.

## 🔍 Simple Example

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\,x^5=5x^4
$$

It also handles roots and reciprocals. For $\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2}$:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\,x^{1/2}=\tfrac{1}{2}x^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}
$$

## ⚠️ Careful

The power rule applies when the **base is the variable** and the exponent is constant, like $x^n$. It does **not** apply to $a^x$ (a constant raised to a variable) — that needs the exponential rule.

## 🔗 Connection

This shortcut is proven from the limit definition. Expanding $(x+h)^n$ with the binomial theorem and letting $h \to 0$ leaves exactly $n x^{n-1}$ — the rule is the definition, just pre-computed once for all powers.

## 🔑 Remember

Bring the power down, then drop it by one: $x^n \to n x^{n-1}$.
