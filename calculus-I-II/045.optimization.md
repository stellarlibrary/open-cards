# Optimization

## 🧠 Big Idea

Optimization means finding the best possible value of a quantity: largest area, smallest cost, shortest distance, greatest volume, and so on.

The calculus part begins after you translate the situation into a function. Then derivatives help locate where the best value can occur.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
Q'(x)=0 \quad \text{or} \quad Q'(x) \text{ does not exist}
$$

Here $Q(x)$ is the quantity being optimized, and $x$ is the variable you chose to describe the situation. The best value can occur at a critical number or at an endpoint of the allowed domain.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine adjusting a slider. As you move it, the quantity you care about rises, falls, or turns around. Optimization asks which slider position gives the best outcome.

The derivative tells where improvement stops changing direction. Endpoints matter too, because sometimes the best choice is at the edge of what is allowed.

## 🔍 Simple Example

A rectangle has perimeter $20$. If one side is $x$, the other is $10-x$, so the area is

$$
A(x)=x(10-x)=10x-x^2
$$

Here $0<x<10$. Since $A'(x)=10-2x$, the critical number is $x=5$. The area is largest when both sides are $5$, giving a square with area $25$.

## ⚠️ Careful

A critical number is only a candidate. Always check the domain and compare all possible candidates, including endpoints when they are included.

## 🔑 Remember

Optimization is modeling first, differentiating second, and comparing candidates last.
