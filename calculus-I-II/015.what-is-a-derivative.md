# What Is a Derivative?

## 🧠 Big Idea

A derivative measures how fast something is changing. It is the **instantaneous rate of change** of a function — how quickly the output responds to a tiny change in the input.

If a limit tells you where a function is heading, a derivative tells you *how steeply* it's moving at a single instant. Speed, growth rate, and slope are all derivatives in disguise.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f'(x)=\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}
$$

This means:

- $f(x+h)-f(x)$: the change in output over a small step
- $h$: the small change in input
- the fraction: average rate of change over that step
- $\lim_{h \to 0}$: shrink the step to zero to get the **instantaneous** rate

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine driving a car. Your average speed over an hour is total distance divided by time. But the speedometer shows your speed *right now* — at this exact instant.

The derivative is the speedometer: it captures the rate of change at one moment, not over a long stretch.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $f(x)=x^2$, the derivative is $f'(x)=2x$.

At $x=3$, $f'(3)=6$. This means that near $x=3$, the output grows about $6$ times as fast as the input — a small nudge in $x$ produces roughly $6$ times that nudge in $f(x)$.

## 🔗 Connection

The derivative is built from a **limit** of average rates of change, so everything you learned about limits powers everything you'll learn about derivatives.

## 🔑 Remember

A derivative is the speedometer of a function: the instantaneous rate of change.
