# Product Rule

## 🧠 Big Idea

The derivative of a product is **not** the product of the derivatives. When two functions are multiplied, each one changes, and both changes contribute to the total rate.

The product rule keeps track of both contributions in one formula.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
(fg)'=f'g+fg'
$$

This means: differentiate the **first** function while keeping the second, then add the first kept while differentiating the **second**. Each term lets one factor change while the other stays put.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture a rectangle with side lengths $f$ and $g$, so the area is $fg$. If both sides grow a little, the area grows in two strips: one from the change in $f$ (width $g$) and one from the change in $g$ (width $f$). Adding those two strips gives $f'g+fg'$.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Differentiate $h(x)=x^2 \sin x$. Let $f=x^2$ and $g=\sin x$:

$$
h'(x)=\underbrace{2x}_{f'}\sin x+x^2\underbrace{\cos x}_{g'}
$$

So $h'(x)=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x$.

## ⚠️ Careful

A common mistake is writing $(fg)'=f'g'$. That's wrong — you must use *both* terms, $f'g+fg'$.

## 🔗 Connection

Use the product rule whenever two variable expressions are **multiplied**, like $x^2 e^x$ or $x\ln x$. If one factor is a plain constant, you don't need it — constants just pull out front.

## 🔑 Remember

Product rule: "first times derivative of second, plus second times derivative of first."
