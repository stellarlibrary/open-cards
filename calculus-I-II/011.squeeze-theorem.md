# Squeeze Theorem

## 🧠 Big Idea

If a function is trapped between two other functions that meet at the same value, then it has no choice but to meet there too. This is the Squeeze Theorem (also called the Sandwich Theorem).

It's powerful for limits you can't compute directly, especially ones involving oscillation.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ near $a$, and

$$
\lim_{x \to a} g(x)=\lim_{x \to a} h(x)=L
$$

then

$$
\lim_{x \to a} f(x)=L
$$

This means: when the lower bound $g$ and upper bound $h$ both head to the same $L$, the function $f$ caught between them must also head to $L$.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine a person squeezed between two walls that are closing in on the same point. Wherever the walls meet, the person must be there too. There's no room to escape.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Find $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\!\frac{1}{x}$.

Since $-1 \le \sin\frac{1}{x} \le 1$, multiply by $x^2 \ge 0$:

$$
-x^2 \le x^2 \sin\frac{1}{x} \le x^2
$$

Both bounds go to $0$ as $x \to 0$, so

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\frac{1}{x}=0
$$

## ⚠️ Careful

You need a **true** bound on both sides near the point, and both bounds must approach the *same* limit. If they disagree, the theorem says nothing.

## 🔑 Remember

Trap the function between two limits that agree, and it's forced to agree too.
