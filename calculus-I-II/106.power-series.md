# Power Series

## 🧠 Big Idea

A power series is an infinite polynomial. Instead of stopping after a few powers of $x$, it keeps going forever with powers like $1,x,x^2,x^3$, and so on.

The important question is not only what the series looks like, but where it actually represents a finite value. A power series may behave like a function for some $x$-values and diverge for others.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n(x-a)^n
$$

This means: for each integer $n$, take the coefficient $c_n$, multiply it by the $n$th power of $x-a$, and add all those terms forever. The number $a$ is the center, and the coefficients $c_n$ control the shape of the series.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a power series as a machine built from simple ingredients: powers of distance from $a$. Near the center, high powers of $x-a$ are usually small, so the early terms often dominate. Farther away, those powers can grow, and the infinite sum may stop making sense.

Power series are powerful because they let us study complicated functions using polynomial-like pieces.

## 🔍 Simple Example

The geometric power series is

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n=1+x+x^2+x^3+\cdots
$$

This adds all nonnegative powers of $x$. When $|x|<1$, the terms shrink enough that the sum equals

$$
\frac{1}{1-x}
$$

So this power series represents the function $\frac{1}{1-x}$ only on the interval where it converges.

## ⚠️ Careful

A power series is not automatically valid for every $x$. Always ask where it converges.

## 🔑 Remember

A power series is an infinite polynomial that only works on its convergence interval.
