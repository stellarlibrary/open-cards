# Direct Comparison Test

## 🧠 Big Idea

The direct comparison test decides convergence by placing an unfamiliar positive series beside a familiar one. If a series is always smaller than a known convergent series, it must converge too. If it is always larger than a known divergent series, it must diverge too.

This test works best when the inequality is clear.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
0\le a_n\le b_n
$$

This means each term of $a_n$ is trapped below the corresponding term of $b_n$. If $\sum b_n$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ also converges.

Similarly, if $0\le b_n\le a_n$ and $\sum b_n$ diverges, then $\sum a_n$ diverges.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine two piles built by adding nonnegative blocks. If the bigger pile has a finite final height, then the smaller pile cannot grow infinitely tall.

On the other hand, if the smaller pile already grows without bound, then the bigger pile must also grow without bound.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2+5}$, notice that

$$
0\le \frac{1}{n^2+5}\le \frac{1}{n^2}
$$

This means each term is no larger than the corresponding term of the convergent $p$-series $\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$. Therefore $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2+5}$ converges.

## ⚠️ Careful

Comparisons must point the right way. Being smaller than a divergent series proves nothing, and being larger than a convergent series proves nothing.

## 🔑 Remember

Compare positive series in the direction that transfers useful information.
