# The Ratio Test

## 🧠 Big Idea

The ratio test studies how one term compares with the next. It is especially powerful for series involving factorials, exponentials, or powers, where neighboring terms simplify nicely.

The test detects whether the tail behaves like a geometric series.

Because it compares neighboring terms, it often removes messy repeated factors that would be hard to judge from the original formula.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
$$

This means $L$ measures the long-run size of the next term relative to the current term. If $L<1$, then $\sum a_n$ converges absolutely. If $L>1$ or $L=\infty$, then $\sum a_n$ diverges. If $L=1$, the test is inconclusive.

## 👀 Intuition

If each new term is eventually less than, say, $80\%$ of the previous one, the tail shrinks like a convergent geometric series. The total remaining amount is finite.

But if terms eventually fail to shrink enough, or grow, the series cannot converge.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}$, use $a_n=\frac1{n!}$.

$$
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|=\frac{1}{n+1}
$$

This means each next term is $\frac{1}{n+1}$ times the previous term. The limit is $0$, so the series converges absolutely by the ratio test.

## ⚠️ Careful

When $L=1$, do not conclude convergence or divergence. Many important series give $L=1$ and need another test.

## 🔑 Remember

The ratio test asks whether neighboring terms eventually shrink like a geometric series.
