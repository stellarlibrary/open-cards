# Polar Graphs

## 🧠 Big Idea

A polar graph is made by letting the distance from the origin depend on the angle.

Instead of choosing an $x$-value and finding a $y$-value, we choose an angle $\theta$ and find how far out to travel. This makes many circular and flower-like curves much simpler to describe.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
r=f(\theta)
$$

This means that for each angle $\theta$, the function gives a radius $r$. To plot a point, rotate to angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis, then move distance $r$ from the origin.

As $\theta$ changes, these points trace the polar curve.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a lighthouse beam rotating around the origin. At each direction, the formula tells how far from the center the curve sits along that beam.

A rectangular graph grows left to right. A polar graph grows by sweeping around an angle, which is why it naturally creates loops, petals, circles, and spirals.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Consider

$$
r=2
$$

This means every point on the graph is exactly $2$ units from the origin, no matter what angle $\theta$ is chosen. As $\theta$ rotates through all directions, the points form a circle of radius $2$ centered at the origin.

## ⚠️ Careful

If $r$ is negative, the point is plotted in the opposite direction from the angle $\theta$. This can make polar graphs look surprising at first.

## 🔑 Remember

A polar graph tells how far out to go at each angle.
