# Limit Comparison Test

## 🧠 Big Idea

The limit comparison test compares two positive series by looking at their long-run term ratio. It is especially useful when formulas look different but grow at the same rate.

Instead of proving term-by-term inequalities, you ask whether the two series have comparable tail behavior.

A good comparison usually comes from keeping only the dominant powers, exponentials, or factorials that control the terms for large $n$.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{b_n}=c, \qquad 0<c<\infty
$$

This means the terms $a_n$ and $b_n$ are eventually the same size up to a constant factor. When this happens, $\sum a_n$ and $\sum b_n$ either both converge or both diverge.

## 👀 Intuition

If two runners stay within a fixed multiple of each other far down the road, then one cannot finish a finite-distance tail while the other runs an infinite-distance tail.

Their early behavior may differ, but convergence is controlled by the tail.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Consider $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{3n+1}{n^3+4}$. For large $n$, the dominant terms suggest comparison with $\frac{1}{n^2}$.

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(3n+1)/(n^3+4)}{1/n^2}=3
$$

This means the given terms behave like $3\cdot\frac{1}{n^2}$ for large $n$. Since $\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges, the original series converges.

## ⚠️ Careful

The standard conclusion requires a positive finite limit. If the limit is $0$ or $\infty$, the test needs one-sided reasoning or a different comparison.

## 🔑 Remember

Limit comparison says same-size positive tails have the same convergence behavior.
