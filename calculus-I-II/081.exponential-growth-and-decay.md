# Exponential Growth and Decay

## 🧠 Big Idea

Exponential growth and decay happen when a quantity changes at a rate proportional to its current size.

If there is more of the quantity, the rate of change is larger. If there is less, the rate is smaller. This makes exponential models useful for populations, radioactive decay, continuously compounded interest, and cooling in simple settings.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{dy}{dt}=ky
$$

This means the rate of change of $y$ with respect to time is $k$ times the current amount $y$. If $k>0$, the quantity grows. If $k<0$, the quantity decays.

$$
y(t)=y_0e^{kt}
$$

This solution means the initial amount $y_0$ is multiplied by the exponential factor $e^{kt}$. The constant $k$ controls how quickly the amount grows or shrinks.

## 👀 Intuition

Exponential change is self-reinforcing. In growth, a larger amount produces more new amount, which then produces even more. In decay, the amount decreases quickly at first, then more slowly because less remains to decay.

The shape is smooth because the change is happening continuously, not in separate jumps.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Suppose bacteria start at $y_0=100$ and grow continuously with $k=0.03$ per hour.

$$
y(t)=100e^{0.03t}
$$

This means the population after $t$ hours is the initial $100$ bacteria multiplied by the continuous growth factor $e^{0.03t}$.

After $5$ hours, $y(5)=100e^{0.15}\approx 116$. The model predicts about $116$ bacteria.

## 🔑 Remember

Exponential models mean the rate of change is proportional to the amount present.
