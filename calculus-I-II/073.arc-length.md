# Arc Length

## 🧠 Big Idea

Arc length measures how far you would travel if you walked along a curve instead of straight across the interval.

A curve is usually longer than its horizontal shadow because it can rise, fall, and bend. Integration lets us add many tiny straight pieces of the curve to get the total length.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

For a smooth curve $y=f(x)$ from $x=a$ to $x=b$,

$$
L=\int_a^b \sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx
$$

This formula says that $L$ is the total length of the curve. The interval $[a,b]$ tells which part of the curve is measured. The derivative $f'(x)$ measures the local steepness, and the factor $\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}$ adjusts each tiny horizontal step $dx$ into a tiny slanted piece of arc length.

## 👀 Intuition

Zoom in closely on a smooth curve. A very small piece looks almost like a straight line segment.

Over a tiny horizontal change $dx$, the vertical change is approximately $dy=f'(x)\,dx$. By the Pythagorean theorem, the tiny length is approximately $\sqrt{dx^2+dy^2}$. Factoring out $dx$ gives the arc length formula.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Find the length of $f(x)=2x$ from $x=0$ to $x=3$. Since $f'(x)=2$,

$$
L=\int_0^3 \sqrt{1+2^2}\,dx=\int_0^3 \sqrt{5}\,dx=3\sqrt{5}
$$

This means the line segment rises fast enough that its true length is $3\sqrt{5}$, not just its horizontal width $3$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Arc length is not area under the curve. It adds tiny distances along the curve, not tiny rectangles beneath it.

## 🔑 Remember

Arc length turns each tiny horizontal step into a tiny slanted distance along the curve.
