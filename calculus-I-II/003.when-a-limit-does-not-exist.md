# When a Limit Does Not Exist

## 🧠 Big Idea

A limit fails to exist when the function has no single value it is heading toward. There are three common ways this happens.

A limit needs **one** clear destination. If the function can't decide, the limit does not exist (DNE).

## 📐 Equation Meaning

The limit does not exist when:

$$
\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) \neq \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)
$$

or the function grows without bound, or it keeps oscillating. In words, the left and right sides disagree, the output blows up, or the output never settles.

## 👀 Intuition

Three ways a journey has no destination:

- **Jump:** the two sides land in different places
- **Blow-up:** the output shoots to $\pm\infty$
- **Wild swinging:** the output oscillates forever and never settles

## 🔍 Simple Example

The step function

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases} 0 & x<0 \\ 1 & x \ge 0 \end{cases}
$$

has

$$
\lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x)=0, \qquad \lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x)=1
$$

Since the two sides disagree, $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$ does not exist.

## ⚠️ Careful

"Does not exist" is different from "equals infinity." Saying the limit is $\infty$ describes *how* it fails; it still does not exist as a finite number.

## 🔑 Remember

No single destination means no limit: jumps, blow-ups, and endless wiggling all fail.
