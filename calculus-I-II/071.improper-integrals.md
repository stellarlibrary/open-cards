# Improper Integrals

## 🧠 Big Idea

An improper integral is a definite integral with an infinite interval or an infinite discontinuity. Since ordinary definite integrals need finite bounds and bounded functions, we define improper integrals using limits.

The answer is not automatically a number. If the limit exists and is finite, the integral converges. If the limit does not exist or becomes infinite, the integral diverges.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx=\lim_{b\to\infty}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
$$

This means we do not really integrate all the way to infinity at once. Instead, we integrate from $a$ to a finite endpoint $b$, then ask what happens as $b$ moves farther and farther right.

For an infinite discontinuity inside an interval, the same idea applies: stop before the problem point, then take a one-sided limit.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine measuring area under a curve that stretches forever. A region can be infinitely long but still have finite area if the curve shrinks fast enough. Improper integrals measure whether the accumulated area settles toward a finite total.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Evaluate

$$
\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx
$$

Rewrite it as a limit:

$$
\lim_{b\to\infty}\int_1^b \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx
$$

The finite integral is

$$
\int_1^b x^{-2}\,dx=\left[-\frac{1}{x}\right]_1^b=1-\frac{1}{b}
$$

So the improper integral equals

$$
\lim_{b\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{b}\right)=1
$$

The limit is finite, so the integral converges.

## 🔑 Remember

An improper integral is a definite integral answered by a limit.
