# The Divergence Test

## 🧠 Big Idea

The divergence test gives a quick way to prove that some infinite series cannot converge. If the terms being added do not approach $0$, the running total has no chance of settling.

For a series to converge, its individual terms must become negligible. This condition is necessary, but it is not enough by itself.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\text{If } \lim_{n\to\infty} a_n \ne 0 \text{ or does not exist, then } \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \text{ diverges.}
$$

This means a nonzero long-term term size forces divergence. If the pieces being added stay away from $0$, the partial sums cannot approach one finite total.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine trying to stop walking while every step remains at least one foot long. You cannot settle at one location if you keep taking steps that do not shrink to zero.

A convergent series needs steps that fade away. Otherwise the total keeps moving too much.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n+1}{n}$, the terms are $a_n=\frac{n+1}{n}=1+\frac1n$.

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n+1}{n}=1
$$

This means the terms approach $1$, not $0$. Therefore the series diverges by the divergence test.

## ⚠️ Careful

If $\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n=0$, the test says nothing. The harmonic series has terms going to $0$ but still diverges.

## 🔑 Remember

Terms not going to $0$ prove divergence; terms going to $0$ prove nothing by themselves.
