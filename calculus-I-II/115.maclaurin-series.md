# Maclaurin Series

## 🧠 Big Idea

A Maclaurin series is a Taylor series centered at $0$. It is not a separate kind of series; it is the special case where the center is the origin.

Maclaurin series are common because many familiar functions are easiest to understand at $x=0$. Values like $e^0$, $\sin 0$, $\cos 0$, and $\ln(1+0)$ are simple, so the derivative data is often easy to compute.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}x^n
$$

This means the function is represented by powers of $x$ instead of powers of $x-a$. The coefficient of $x^n$ comes from the $n$th derivative of $f$ at $0$, divided by $n!$.

A Maclaurin series is exactly the Taylor formula with $a=0$.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of the origin as the home base. A Maclaurin series describes how a function behaves as you move away from $0$ to the left or right.

Because the powers are $x^n$, the formula often looks cleaner than a Taylor series centered somewhere else. But the same convergence questions still apply.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $f(x)=\cos x$, the derivatives cycle: $\cos x$, $-\sin x$, $-\cos x$, $\sin x$, then repeat. At $0$, the nonzero values occur in the even derivatives. The Maclaurin series is

$$
\cos x=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^n\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}
$$

This means $\cos x=1-\frac{x^2}{2!}+\frac{x^4}{4!}-\cdots$, with only even powers appearing.

## 🔗 Connection

The series for $e^x$, $\sin x$, and $\cos x$ are among the most important Maclaurin series to remember.

## 🔑 Remember

Maclaurin means Taylor centered at zero.
