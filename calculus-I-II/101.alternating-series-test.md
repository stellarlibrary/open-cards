# Alternating Series Test

## 🧠 Big Idea

The alternating series test handles series whose signs switch back and forth. Alternation can make a series converge even when the positive version would diverge, because positive and negative terms partially cancel.

The cancellation is reliable when the term sizes decrease toward $0$.

Then each partial sum overshoots the final value by a smaller amount, so the running totals get squeezed into place.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}b_n \text{ converges if } b_n\downarrow 0
$$

This means the series converges when $b_n>0$, the terms $b_n$ are eventually decreasing, and $\lim_{n\to\infty}b_n=0$. The factor $(-1)^{n+1}$ creates the alternating signs.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine walking past a target: first too far to the right, then back to the left, then right again, with each step smaller than the last. If the step sizes shrink to zero, your position gets trapped closer and closer to one point.

That trapping is the heart of alternating convergence.

## 🔍 Simple Example

The alternating harmonic series is

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{1}{n}
$$

This means the terms are $1,-\frac12,\frac13,-\frac14,\dots$. Here $b_n=\frac1n$ is positive, decreasing, and approaches $0$, so the series converges by the alternating series test.

## ⚠️ Careful

Alternation alone is not enough. The positive term sizes must decrease to $0$, and convergence here may be conditional rather than absolute.

## 🔑 Remember

Alternating signs converge when the shrinking term sizes march steadily down to $0$.
