# Separable Equations

## 🧠 Big Idea

A separable differential equation is one where the $x$-parts and $y$-parts can be moved to opposite sides of the equation.

Once separated, the equation becomes an integration problem. The goal is to collect all terms involving $y$ with $dy$, collect all terms involving $x$ with $dx$, and then integrate both sides.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=g(x)h(y)
$$

This means the rate of change of $y$ can be written as a product of one factor depending on $x$ and one factor depending on $y$.

$$
\frac{1}{h(y)}\,dy=g(x)\,dx
$$

This means the $y$-dependent part has been placed with $dy$, while the $x$-dependent part has been placed with $dx$.

$$
\int \frac{1}{h(y)}\,dy=\int g(x)\,dx
$$

This means we integrate both sides to find an equation relating $x$ and $y$.

## 👀 Intuition

Separation is like sorting mixed objects into two baskets. All the $y$ objects go on one side, and all the $x$ objects go on the other.

After the variables are sorted, integration rebuilds the relationship between them.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $\frac{dy}{dx}=xy$, separate variables:

$$
\frac{1}{y}\,dy=x\,dx
$$

This means $y$ is paired with $dy$, while $x$ is paired with $dx$.

Integrating gives $\ln |y|=\frac{x^2}{2}+C$, so solutions can be written as $y=Ce^{x^2/2}$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Dividing by $h(y)$ can sometimes hide constant solutions where $h(y)=0$, so always check for them.

## 🔑 Remember

Separate variables first, then integrate both sides.
