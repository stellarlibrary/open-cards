# Parametric Derivatives

## 🧠 Big Idea

A parametric derivative finds the slope of a curve when $x$ and $y$ are both controlled by a parameter.

The curve may not be written as $y=f(x)$, but slope still means vertical change compared with horizontal change. Since both changes happen through $t$, we compare their rates with respect to $t$.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\frac{dy}{dt}}{\frac{dx}{dt}}
$$

This means the slope with respect to $x$ equals the rate at which $y$ changes with $t$ divided by the rate at which $x$ changes with $t$. The formula applies when $\frac{dx}{dt}\ne 0$.

It is the chain rule idea in reverse: vertical speed divided by horizontal speed gives slope.

## 👀 Intuition

Think about a moving car on a curved road. Its vertical speed tells how fast it moves north or south. Its horizontal speed tells how fast it moves east or west.

If the car moves upward quickly while moving right slowly, the path is steep. If it moves right quickly while barely changing height, the path is flatter.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let

$$
x=t^2+1, \qquad y=t^3
$$

This means $x$ and $y$ both change as $t$ changes. Their rates are

$$
\frac{dx}{dt}=2t, \qquad \frac{dy}{dt}=3t^2
$$

These equations say the horizontal speed is $2t$ and the vertical speed is $3t^2$. Therefore,

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3t^2}{2t}=\frac{3t}{2} \quad (t\ne 0)
$$

At $t=2$, the slope is $3$, meaning the curve rises about $3$ units for each unit to the right at that point.

## 🔑 Remember

For parametric curves, slope is vertical speed divided by horizontal speed.
