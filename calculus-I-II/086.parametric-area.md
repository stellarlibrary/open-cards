# Parametric Area

## 🧠 Big Idea

Parametric area measures the area swept under a curve when the curve is described by a parameter instead of directly by $y=f(x)$.

The usual area formula $\int y\,dx$ still works. The only difference is that $dx$ must be translated into parameter language using how $x$ changes with $t$.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
A=\int_a^b y(t)x'(t)\,dt
$$

This means the height of the curve is $y(t)$, while $x'(t)\,dt$ represents a tiny horizontal change $dx$. Multiplying them gives a thin area slice, and integrating adds the slices from $t=a$ to $t=b$.

This formula gives signed area, so the setup should match the direction of motion. If $x$ decreases or the curve crosses below the axis, you may need to adjust limits or take absolute area.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture the moving point dragging a vertical curtain down to the $x$-axis. When the point moves a tiny amount horizontally, it sweeps a thin rectangle-like strip.

The strip's height is the current $y$-value, and its width is the tiny change in $x$.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let

$$
x=t, \qquad y=t^2, \qquad 0\le t\le 2
$$

This means the parameter is exactly the $x$-coordinate, so the curve is $y=x^2$ from $x=0$ to $x=2$. Since $x'(t)=1$,

$$
A=\int_0^2 t^2\,dt=\frac{8}{3}
$$

The area under the curve and above the $x$-axis is $\frac{8}{3}$ square units.

## 🔑 Remember

Parametric area is still height times horizontal change, written in terms of $t$.
