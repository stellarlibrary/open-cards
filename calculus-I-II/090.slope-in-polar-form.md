# Slope in Polar Form

## 🧠 Big Idea

Slope in polar form measures how steep a polar curve is after translating its motion into $x$- and $y$-coordinates.

A polar equation gives radius as a function of angle, not $y$ as a function of $x$. To find $\frac{dy}{dx}$, we treat the polar curve like a parametric curve with parameter $\theta$.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\frac{dr}{d\theta}\sin\theta+r\cos\theta}{\frac{dr}{d\theta}\cos\theta-r\sin\theta}
$$

This means the slope equals vertical change with respect to $\theta$ divided by horizontal change with respect to $\theta$. It comes from using $x=r\cos\theta$ and $y=r\sin\theta$, then applying the parametric derivative formula.

The numerator is $\frac{dy}{d\theta}$, and the denominator is $\frac{dx}{d\theta}$.

## 👀 Intuition

As $\theta$ changes, a polar point can move because the angle rotates, because the radius changes, or both. The tangent direction depends on the combined effect of those two motions.

So polar slope is not just about whether $r$ is increasing. It is about how the moving point travels in the plane.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For the circle

$$
r=2
$$

This means $\frac{dr}{d\theta}=0$, because the radius is constant. The slope becomes

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2\cos\theta}{-2\sin\theta}=-\cot\theta
$$

At $\theta=\pi/4$, the slope is $-1$, matching the downward tangent direction on the circle at that point.

## ⚠️ Careful

If the denominator is $0$, the tangent may be vertical rather than having an ordinary finite slope.

## 🔑 Remember

Polar slope is parametric slope with $\theta$ as the parameter.
