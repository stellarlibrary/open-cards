# One-Sided Limits

## 🧠 Big Idea

Sometimes a function approaches different values depending on the direction you come from. A one-sided limit looks at only one side of the point.

We can approach $a$ from the left (smaller values) or from the right (larger values), and these two journeys may end in different places.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) \qquad \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)
$$

This means:

- $x \to a^-$: $x$ approaches $a$ from the left, using values **less than** $a$
- $x \to a^+$: $x$ approaches $a$ from the right, using values **greater than** $a$

The little $-$ and $+$ signs tell you which side you are walking in from.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture a staircase. As you walk up to a step from the left, you land at one height. Coming from the right, you might land at a different height.

The two-sided limit exists only when both sides agree on the same destination.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=\dfrac{|x|}{x}$.

For $x>0$, $f(x)=1$, so

$$
\lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x)=1
$$

For $x<0$, $f(x)=-1$, so

$$
\lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x)=-1
$$

The sides disagree, so the two-sided limit at $0$ does not exist.

## 🔗 Connection

The full limit exists only when

$$
\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)=\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)
$$

Both one-sided limits must agree.

## 🔑 Remember

A two-sided limit exists only when the left and right sides head to the same value.
