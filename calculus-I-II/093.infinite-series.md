# Infinite Series

## 🧠 Big Idea

An infinite series is what you get when you add the terms of an infinite sequence. Since you cannot literally finish infinitely many additions, calculus studies the running totals.

The key question is whether those running totals approach a finite number. If they do, the series converges. If they do not, the series diverges.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n
$$

This means an infinite series is defined by the limit of its partial sums. The expression $\sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n$ adds only the first $N$ terms, and then we ask what happens as $N$ grows forever.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of pouring water into a cup in smaller and smaller amounts. Even if you pour infinitely many times, the cup may approach a fixed fill level. That fixed level is like a convergent series.

But if the added amounts do not shrink fast enough, the total keeps growing or oscillating and the series diverges.

## 🔍 Simple Example

The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n}$ has partial sums $\frac12,\frac34,\frac78,\dots$.

These totals get closer to $1$, so the infinite series converges to $1$. The individual terms keep being added, but the remaining gap keeps shrinking.

## ⚠️ Careful

A series is not the same thing as a sequence. The sequence gives the terms $a_n$; the series studies the sums of those terms.

## 🔑 Remember

An infinite series converges when its partial sums settle toward a finite total.
