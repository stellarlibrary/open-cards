# Sequence Convergence

## 🧠 Big Idea

A sequence converges when its terms settle toward one fixed number. The early terms may bounce around, but the long-run behavior becomes predictable.

Convergence is about the tail of the sequence, not about the first few terms. Changing finitely many starting terms does not change whether a sequence converges.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n=L
$$

This means that as the index $n$ grows without bound, the terms $a_n$ get closer and closer to the number $L$. The number $L$ is called the limit of the sequence.

If no such finite number exists, the sequence diverges.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine dropping a ball that bounces lower each time. The bounce heights form a sequence. If the heights approach the floor level, the sequence converges to that height.

The sequence does not need to reach the limit exactly. It only needs to get arbitrarily close and stay close eventually.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $a_n=\frac{3n+1}{n}$, divide each term by $n$:

$$
a_n=3+\frac{1}{n}
$$

This says every term is $3$ plus a small extra piece. As $n$ gets large, $\frac{1}{n}$ gets close to $0$, so $a_n$ gets close to $3$.

Therefore the sequence converges to $3$.

## ⚠️ Careful

A sequence can be bounded without converging. For example, $(-1)^n$ stays between $-1$ and $1$, but it never settles on one value.

## 🔑 Remember

A sequence converges when its far-out terms settle toward one number.
