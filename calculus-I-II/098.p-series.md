# p-Series

## 🧠 Big Idea

A $p$-series is a basic benchmark series whose convergence depends on how fast powers of $n$ grow in the denominator. It is one of the most important comparison tools in Calculus II.

The larger $p$ is, the faster the terms shrink. The dividing line is exactly $p=1$.

That boundary is important because it separates terms that shrink merely to zero from terms that shrink fast enough to make a finite total.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^p}\text{ converges if and only if }p>1
$$

This means the infinite sum is finite when the exponent $p$ is greater than $1$, and it diverges when $p\le 1$. The condition covers all real values of $p$.

## 👀 Intuition

The terms $\frac{1}{n^p}$ always shrink when $p>0$, but shrinking is not automatically fast enough. The harmonic series, where $p=1$, shrinks too slowly and diverges.

Once $p$ is bigger than $1$, the tail becomes thin enough that the total remaining amount is finite.

## 🔍 Simple Example

The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^3}$ is a $p$-series with $p=3$.

Since $3>1$, the series converges. We do not need to know its exact sum to know its convergence behavior.

By contrast, $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}$ has $p=1$, so it diverges.

## 🔗 Connection

Many comparison tests work by comparing an unfamiliar series to a familiar $p$-series.

## 🔑 Remember

A $p$-series converges exactly when the power is greater than $1$.
