# The Shell Method

## 🧠 Big Idea

The shell method finds volumes of solids of revolution by adding thin cylindrical shells.

Instead of slicing perpendicular to the axis of rotation, shells usually come from slices parallel to the axis. Each slice rotates into a hollow cylinder with a radius, a height, and a tiny thickness.

This method is especially helpful when disks or washers would require solving curves for the other variable.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
V=\int_a^b 2\pi x\,h(x)\,dx
$$

This formula is for rotation around the $y$-axis with $x\ge 0$. Here $V$ is volume, $x$ is the shell radius, $h(x)$ is the shell height, and $dx$ is the shell thickness. The factor $2\pi x$ is the circumference of the shell.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine peeling a label off a soup can. The label is almost a rectangle: circumference times height.

A cylindrical shell is like that label with a tiny thickness. Its volume is approximately circumference times height times thickness, and the integral adds all the shells.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Rotate the region under $y=4-x^2$ from $x=0$ to $x=2$ around the $y$-axis.

A shell at position $x$ has radius $x$ and height $4-x^2$. The volume is $\int_0^2 2\pi x(4-x^2)\,dx=8\pi$. Each shell wraps around the $y$-axis and contributes a thin layer of volume.

## ⚠️ Careful

If the rotation axis is not the $y$-axis, the radius is the distance to that axis, not necessarily $x$.

## 🔑 Remember

Shell method: circumference times height times thickness.
