# Logistic Growth

## 🧠 Big Idea

Logistic growth models a population that grows quickly at first but slows down as it approaches a maximum sustainable size.

Unlike exponential growth, logistic growth includes a limiting factor. The environment can only support so many individuals, so the population approaches a carrying capacity instead of increasing forever.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{dP}{dt}=kP\left(1-\frac{P}{M}\right)
$$

This means the population $P$ changes at a rate depending on both its current size and how close it is to the carrying capacity $M$. The constant $k>0$ controls the natural growth rate, while $1-\frac{P}{M}$ slows growth as $P$ gets near $M$.

$$
P(t)=\frac{M}{1+Ae^{-kt}}
$$

This solution means the population approaches $M$ over time. The constant $A$ depends on the initial population, and as $t$ increases, $e^{-kt}$ moves toward $0$, making $P(t)$ move toward $M$.

## 👀 Intuition

At first, when the population is small, resources are plentiful and growth looks almost exponential. Later, crowding, food limits, or space limits reduce the growth rate.

The graph often has an S-shape: slow start, fast middle growth, then leveling off near the carrying capacity.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Suppose a lake can support $M=1000$ fish, with $k=0.4$, and the initial population is $100$. Then $A=9$, so

$$
P(t)=\frac{1000}{1+9e^{-0.4t}}
$$

This means the fish population starts at $100$, increases over time, and gets closer and closer to $1000$ without passing that long-term limit in the model.

## 🔑 Remember

Logistic growth is exponential growth slowed by a carrying capacity.
