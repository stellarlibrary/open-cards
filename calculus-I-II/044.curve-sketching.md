# Curve Sketching

## 🧠 Big Idea

Curve sketching uses calculus clues to draw a graph that matches the function's real behavior. Instead of plotting many random points, you organize the graph by intercepts, increasing and decreasing intervals, local extrema, concavity, and inflection points.

The goal is not artistic perfection. The goal is a faithful picture of the function's shape.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f(x) \text{ gives height}, \qquad f'(x) \text{ gives slope}, \qquad f''(x) \text{ gives bending}
$$

The function $f$ tells where the graph is. The first derivative $f'$ tells whether it rises or falls. The second derivative $f''$ tells whether it bends up or down.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of building a map. Intercepts mark landmarks, critical numbers mark possible peaks and valleys, and concavity shows the curve of the road between them.

A good sketch connects these clues smoothly, respecting what each derivative says.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $f(x)=x^3-3x$,

$$
f'(x)=3x^2-3, \qquad f''(x)=6x
$$

The first derivative shows a local maximum at $x=-1$ and a local minimum at $x=1$. The second derivative shows concave down for $x<0$ and concave up for $x>0$. These facts give the main shape of the graph.

## ⚠️ Careful

Do not let a rough sketch contradict derivative information. If $f'(x)>0$ on an interval, the graph should not be falling there.

## 🔑 Remember

Curve sketching turns derivative information into a reliable picture.
