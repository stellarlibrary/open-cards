# Integration by Parts

## 🧠 Big Idea

Integration by parts is the integration version of the product rule. It helps when an integrand is a product and one factor becomes simpler when differentiated.

The method trades one integral for another. The goal is not merely to apply the formula, but to choose $u$ and $dv$ so that the new integral is easier than the original one.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du
$$

Here $u$ is the part you choose to differentiate, so $du$ is its derivative. The piece $dv$ is the part you choose to integrate, so $v$ is its antiderivative. The formula says: product $uv$, minus a new integral built from $v$ and $du$.

## 👀 Intuition

Some products are hard because two factors are tangled together. Integration by parts shifts the derivative from one factor to the other. If the chosen $u$ becomes simpler, the product becomes less tangled.

A common useful choice is to let $u$ be a polynomial, logarithm, or inverse trig function, because those often simplify when differentiated.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Find

$$
\int x e^x\,dx
$$

Choose $u=x$ because it becomes simpler, and choose $dv=e^x\,dx$ because it is easy to integrate. Then $du=dx$ and $v=e^x$. So

$$
\int x e^x\,dx=xe^x-\int e^x\,dx=xe^x-e^x+C
$$

This means the antiderivative is $e^x(x-1)+C$.

## ⚠️ Careful

A poor choice of $u$ can make the new integral harder. The formula is correct, but strategy still matters.

## 🔑 Remember

Integration by parts moves work from one factor to another: $\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du$.
