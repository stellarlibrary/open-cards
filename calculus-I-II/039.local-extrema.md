# Local Extrema

## 🧠 Big Idea

A local extremum is a high or low point compared with nearby points. A local maximum is locally highest; a local minimum is locally lowest.

The word **local** matters. The point does not have to be the highest or lowest on the entire graph, only in a small neighborhood around it.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f(c)\ge f(x) \text{ near } c \quad \text{or} \quad f(c)\le f(x) \text{ near } c
$$

The first inequality describes a local maximum: $f(c)$ is at least as large as nearby output values. The second describes a local minimum: $f(c)$ is at most as large as nearby output values.

At an interior point, if $f$ has a local extremum at $c$ and $f'(c)$ exists, then Fermat's theorem says $f'(c)=0$.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture a hilly trail. A local maximum is a hilltop compared with the ground immediately around it. A local minimum is a valley bottom compared with the nearby ground.

There may be taller mountains or deeper valleys elsewhere. Local extrema only compare nearby behavior.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^2$. At $x=0$,

$$
f(0)=0
$$

This value is less than nearby values like $f(1)=1$ and $f(-1)=1$. So $x=0$ gives a local minimum. In fact, it is also the absolute minimum.

## ⚠️ Careful

Not every critical number is a local extremum. For $f(x)=x^3$, the derivative is zero at $x=0$, but the graph keeps increasing through that point.

## 🔑 Remember

Local extrema are nearby highs and lows, not necessarily overall highs and lows.
