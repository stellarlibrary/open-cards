# Continuity Has Three Conditions

## 🧠 Big Idea

A function is continuous at a point when you can draw through it without lifting your pen. Formally, three separate conditions must all hold.

Continuity ties together the function's value and its limit: where the function is heading must match where it actually is.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$f$ is continuous at $x=a$ when all three hold:

$$
1)\; f(a) \text{ is defined} \qquad 2)\; \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \text{ exists} \qquad 3)\; \lim_{x \to a} f(x)=f(a)
$$

This means: the point exists, the function approaches a single value, and that value is exactly the function's height there. If any one fails, the function is discontinuous at $a$.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a smooth path with no holes, no gaps, and no sudden jumps. Condition 1 says the point is there; condition 2 says the function aims at one spot; condition 3 says it actually lands on that spot.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let

$$
f(x)=\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}
$$

At $x=1$, $f(1)$ is undefined, so condition 1 fails. Even though $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)=2$ exists, the function is **not** continuous at $x=1$ — there's a hole.

## ⚠️ Careful

A limit existing is not enough. The function value must also exist *and* equal that limit. All three conditions are required.

## 🔑 Remember

Continuous = defined, limit exists, and they match — all three, no exceptions.
