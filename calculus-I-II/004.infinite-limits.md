# Infinite Limits

## 🧠 Big Idea

An infinite limit describes a function that grows without bound near a point. The output races toward $+\infty$ or $-\infty$ instead of settling on a number.

This usually happens when the denominator of a fraction shrinks toward zero while the top stays away from zero.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\lim_{x \to a} f(x)=\infty
$$

This means: as $x$ approaches $a$, the output $f(x)$ becomes larger than any number you can name. It does **not** mean the limit equals a real number $\infty$; it describes unbounded growth.

A vertical line $x=a$ where this happens is called a **vertical asymptote**.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of dividing $1$ by a tinier and tinier positive number: $1/0.1=10$, $1/0.01=100$, $1/0.001=1000$. The smaller the bottom, the bigger the result. As the denominator approaches zero, the value explodes upward.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Consider

$$
f(x)=\frac{1}{x^2}
$$

As $x \to 0$, the denominator $x^2$ becomes a tiny positive number, so

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x^2}=\infty
$$

The graph has a vertical asymptote at $x=0$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Check the sign on each side. For $\dfrac{1}{x}$, the left side goes to $-\infty$ and the right side to $+\infty$, so the two-sided limit does not exist.

## 🔑 Remember

A small denominator with a nonzero top sends the function to infinity, creating a vertical asymptote.
