# The Disk Method

## 🧠 Big Idea

The disk method finds the volume of a solid formed by rotating a region around an axis.

When each cross-section perpendicular to the axis of rotation is a solid circle, the slices are disks. The volume comes from adding up the areas of those circular disks.

This is really the slicing method with a special cross-sectional area: the area of a circle.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
V=\int_a^b \pi [R(x)]^2\,dx
$$

Here $V$ is volume, $[a,b]$ is the interval of rotation, and $R(x)$ is the radius of the disk at position $x$. Since each slice is a circle, its area is $\pi [R(x)]^2$, and $dx$ is the thin slice thickness.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine stacking many extremely thin coins. Each coin has a circular face, but the radius can change from one position to the next.

Rotating a curve around an axis creates exactly this kind of stack. The farther the curve is from the axis, the larger the disk radius becomes.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Rotate the region under $y=x$ from $x=0$ to $x=2$ around the $x$-axis.

At each $x$, the radius is the distance from the axis to the curve, so $R(x)=x$. The volume is $\int_0^2 \pi x^2\,dx=\frac{8\pi}{3}$. This gives the volume of the cone formed by the rotation.

## ⚠️ Careful

The radius is always a distance from the axis of rotation, not just automatically the function value.

## 🔑 Remember

Disk method: rotate a region with no hole, then integrate $\pi R^2$.
