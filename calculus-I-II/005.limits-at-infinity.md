# Limits at Infinity

## 🧠 Big Idea

A limit at infinity asks what happens to a function as the input grows very large. Instead of zooming in on a point, we zoom out to see the long-run behavior.

This tells us the function's **end behavior** and reveals horizontal asymptotes.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x)=L
$$

This means: as $x$ grows without bound, the output $f(x)$ settles toward the value $L$. If this happens, the line $y=L$ is a **horizontal asymptote**.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture standing on a long straight highway and looking far into the distance. Nearby bumps stop mattering; what matters is the overall direction the road is settling into.

For rational functions, the highest-power terms dominate when $x$ is huge, so smaller terms fade away.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Consider

$$
f(x)=\frac{3x^2+5}{x^2-1}
$$

Divide top and bottom by $x^2$:

$$
\frac{3+\frac{5}{x^2}}{1-\frac{1}{x^2}}
$$

As $x \to \infty$, the fractions $\frac{5}{x^2}$ and $\frac{1}{x^2}$ vanish, leaving

$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x)=\frac{3}{1}=3
$$

So $y=3$ is a horizontal asymptote.

## ⚠️ Careful

For a rational function, compare the degrees of top and bottom:

- top degree **<** bottom degree: limit is $0$
- top degree **=** bottom degree: limit is the ratio of leading coefficients
- top degree **>** bottom degree: no horizontal asymptote (limit is $\pm\infty$)

## 🔑 Remember

Limits at infinity reveal end behavior: keep the dominant terms and let the small ones vanish.
