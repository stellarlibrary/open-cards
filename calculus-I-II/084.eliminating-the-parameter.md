# Eliminating the Parameter

## 🧠 Big Idea

Eliminating the parameter means rewriting parametric equations as one equation involving only $x$ and $y$.

This helps reveal the familiar Cartesian shape of the curve. But it can also throw away information, especially the direction the curve is traced and any restrictions caused by the parameter.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
x=f(t), \qquad y=g(t) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad F(x,y)=0
$$

This means we start with both coordinates depending on $t$, then algebraically remove $t$ to get a relationship between $x$ and $y$ alone. The equation $F(x,y)=0$ describes the same set of points when no domain restrictions are lost.

The goal is not to differentiate or integrate yet. The goal is to see the curve's ordinary shape.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine receiving directions for a trip minute by minute: east position and north position as functions of time. Eliminating the parameter is like drawing the final road on a map without writing the clock times on it.

The map shows where the traveler went, but not how fast or in which direction.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let

$$
x=t+1, \qquad y=t^2
$$

This means $t=x-1$ from the first equation. Substituting into the second equation gives

$$
y=(x-1)^2
$$

This means the parametric curve lies on the parabola $y=(x-1)^2$. If all real $t$ are allowed, all real $x$ occur too.

## ⚠️ Careful

Always check parameter restrictions. If $t\ge 0$, the same algebra would give only the right half of the parabola, where $x\ge 1$.

## 🔑 Remember

Eliminating the parameter shows the shape, but not always the whole story.
