# Derivatives of Exponential Functions

## 🧠 Big Idea

The exponential function $e^x$ is the superstar of calculus: it is its **own derivative**. No other function (besides multiples of it) has this property.

This is exactly why $e$ is the "natural" base — it makes growth and its rate of change line up perfectly.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\,e^x=e^x
$$

This means: the slope of $e^x$ at any point equals the height of $e^x$ at that point. For a general base $a$:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\,a^x=a^x \ln a
$$

The extra factor $\ln a$ is the price you pay for not using the natural base $e$ (where $\ln e=1$).

## 👀 Intuition

Exponential growth means the rate of increase is proportional to the current amount — the more you have, the faster you grow. For base $e$, that proportionality constant is exactly $1$, so growth equals size.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Differentiate $h(x)=e^{3x}$ with the chain rule:

$$
h'(x)=e^{3x}\cdot 3=3e^{3x}
$$

## ⚠️ Careful

Don't confuse $e^x$ with a power like $x^e$. The power rule is for a **variable base**; the exponential rule is for a **variable exponent**. They are completely different.

## 🔗 Connection

Being its own derivative is exactly why $e^x$ models **natural growth and decay** — populations, interest, radioactivity. Any quantity whose rate is proportional to its size is governed by an exponential, the solution to $y'=ky$.

## 🔑 Remember

$e^x$ is its own derivative — the rate of natural growth equals its current size.
