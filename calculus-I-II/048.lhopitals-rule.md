# L'Hôpital's Rule

## 🧠 Big Idea

L'Hôpital's Rule helps evaluate certain difficult limits of quotients. When direct substitution gives the indeterminate form $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$, the original quotient may be replaced by a quotient of derivatives.

The rule works because derivatives compare how fast the numerator and denominator approach their limiting behavior.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

For an indeterminate quotient of type $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$,

$$
\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}
$$

This means the limit of the original quotient equals the limit of the quotient of derivatives, if the derivative quotient limit exists. The functions must be differentiable near $a$, with $g'(x)\ne 0$ near $a$ where needed.

## 👀 Intuition

If both numerator and denominator are shrinking to $0$, their actual sizes may be hard to compare. Their derivatives measure their local speeds. L'Hôpital's Rule says that, under the right conditions, comparing speeds gives the same limiting ratio.

For $\infty/\infty$, it compares how fast both quantities grow.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Evaluate

$$
\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}
$$

Direct substitution gives $0/0$, so L'Hôpital's Rule applies. Differentiate the numerator and denominator separately:

$$
\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\cos x}{1}=1
$$

Therefore the original limit is $1$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Use L'Hôpital's Rule only for indeterminate quotients $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$. It does not apply just because a limit contains $0$ or infinity.

## 🔑 Remember

For $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$, compare derivative rates, not original sizes.
