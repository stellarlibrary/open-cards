# Fluid Force

## 🧠 Big Idea

Fluid force measures the total push of a liquid against a submerged surface.

The deeper a point is below the surface, the greater the pressure there. Integration is needed because pressure is not constant on a vertical object: the bottom usually feels more push than the top.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

For a vertical plate divided into thin horizontal strips,

$$
F=\int_a^b \rho g\,h(y)\,w(y)\,dy
$$

Here $F$ is the total fluid force. The product $\rho g$ is the fluid's weight density, such as about $9800\,\text{N/m}^3$ for fresh water. The depth $h(y)$ tells how far the strip is below the surface, so $\rho g h(y)$ is the pressure on that strip. The product $w(y)\,dy$ is the strip's tiny area.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine a dam wall. Water near the top presses lightly because there is little water above it. Water near the bottom presses harder because it supports the weight of all the water above.

The integral adds pressure times area across the whole surface, strip by strip.

## 🔍 Simple Example

A rectangular plate is $2$ meters wide and $3$ meters tall, with its top edge at the water surface. Measuring depth $y$ downward, $0\le y\le 3$, we have $w(y)=2$ and $h(y)=y$.

$$
F=\int_0^3 9800y(2)\,dy=19600\left.\frac{y^2}{2}\right|_0^3=88200
$$

The total force is $88200$ newtons, with the lower strips contributing more than the upper strips.

## ⚠️ Careful

Do not use the same pressure everywhere unless the surface is horizontal at one depth. For vertical surfaces, depth changes across the plate.

## 🔑 Remember

Fluid force is pressure times tiny area, added over a submerged surface.
