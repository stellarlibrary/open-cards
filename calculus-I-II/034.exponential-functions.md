# Exponential Functions

## 🧠 Big Idea

An exponential function changes by repeated multiplication.

In a linear function, equal steps in the input add equal amounts to the output. In an exponential function, equal steps in the input multiply the output by the same factor. That is why exponentials model growth, decay, interest, and many natural processes.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f(x)=a\,b^x, \qquad b>0,\ b\neq 1
$$

This means $a$ is the starting value when $x=0$, and $b$ is the factor applied for each increase of $1$ in $x$. If $b>1$, the function grows. If $0<b<1$, the function decays.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a population that grows by $5\%$ each year. It does not gain the same number of people every year. Instead, it multiplies by $1.05$ each year, so the increases themselves get larger as the population grows.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Suppose an investment starts at $100$ dollars and grows by $5\%$ per year. A model is

$$
A(t)=100(1.05)^t
$$

This means $100$ is the initial amount, $1.05$ is the yearly growth factor, and $t$ is time in years.

After $2$ years,

$$
A(2)=100(1.05)^2=110.25
$$

This says the investment is worth $110.25$ dollars after two yearly growth steps.

## 🔗 Connection

The natural base $e$ is especially important in calculus because it matches continuous growth.

## 🔑 Remember

Exponential change means equal input steps multiply the output by a constant factor.
