# Work

## 🧠 Big Idea

Work measures how much effort a force spends while moving an object through a distance.

When the force is constant, work is simply force times distance. When the force changes from place to place, integration adds up many tiny pieces of work.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

If a force $F(x)$ acts along the direction of motion from $x=a$ to $x=b$, then

$$
W=\int_a^b F(x)\,dx
$$

This formula says that total work $W$ is accumulated force over distance. The function $F(x)$ gives the force at position $x$. The small piece $F(x)\,dx$ represents approximately constant force over a tiny distance, and the integral adds those pieces from $a$ to $b$.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture pushing a cart along a track. If the track gets steeper, the force needed may increase. Instead of pretending the force is the same everywhere, calculus slices the trip into tiny intervals where the force is almost constant.

Each tiny interval contributes a little work. The total work is the sum of all those contributions.

## 🔍 Simple Example

A spring requires force $F(x)=3x$ newtons to stretch it $x$ meters from its natural length. The work to stretch it from $0$ to $2$ meters is

$$
W=\int_0^2 3x\,dx=\left.\frac{3x^2}{2}\right|_0^2=6
$$

So the spring requires $6$ joules of work. The force starts at $0$ and grows as the spring stretches.

## ⚠️ Careful

Use the component of force in the direction of motion. A force at an angle may do less work than its full magnitude suggests.

## 🔑 Remember

Work is accumulated force over distance.
