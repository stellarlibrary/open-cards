# Surface Area of Revolution

## 🧠 Big Idea

Surface area of revolution measures the area swept out when a curve is rotated around an axis.

Instead of adding flat rectangles, we add many tiny circular bands. Each band has a circumference determined by its distance from the axis and a slanted width determined by arc length.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

For a nonnegative smooth curve $y=f(x)$ rotated around the $x$-axis from $x=a$ to $x=b$,

$$
S=\int_a^b 2\pi f(x)\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx
$$

Here $S$ is the surface area. The factor $2\pi f(x)$ is the circumference of the circular path traced by the point $(x,f(x))$. The factor $\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx$ is a tiny arc length piece along the curve. The integral adds the areas of all those thin bands.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine wrapping a very thin strip around the outside of a vase. The strip's length around the vase is a circumference, and its width is the tiny slanted length of the curve that generated it.

Adding all those narrow strips gives the surface area. This is why the formula combines a circular idea with the arc length idea.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Rotate $f(x)=1$ from $x=0$ to $x=3$ around the $x$-axis. Since $f'(x)=0$,

$$
S=\int_0^3 2\pi(1)\sqrt{1+0^2}\,dx=\int_0^3 2\pi\,dx=6\pi
$$

This gives the lateral surface area of a cylinder with radius $1$ and height $3$.

## ⚠️ Careful

This formula is for rotation around the $x$-axis with $f(x)\ge 0$. For another axis, the radius must be the distance from the curve to that axis.

## 🔑 Remember

Surface area of revolution is circumference times tiny arc length, added all along the curve.
