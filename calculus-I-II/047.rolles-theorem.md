# Rolle's Theorem

## 🧠 Big Idea

Rolle's Theorem says that if a smooth enough function starts and ends at the same height, then somewhere in between it must have a horizontal tangent.

It is a special case of the Mean Value Theorem where the average rate of change is zero.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, and $f(a)=f(b)$, then there is some $c$ in $(a,b)$ such that

$$
f'(c)=0
$$

The condition $f(a)=f(b)$ means the secant line from the start to the end is horizontal. The conclusion $f'(c)=0$ means at least one tangent line inside the interval is also horizontal.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine throwing a ball that returns to the same height. If its height changes smoothly, then at some instant between the two equal heights it must either level off or turn around.

That level or turning instant has horizontal tangent slope.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^2-1$ on $[-1,1]$. Then

$$
f(-1)=0 \quad \text{and} \quad f(1)=0
$$

The endpoints have the same height. Since $f$ is a polynomial, it is continuous and differentiable. Rolle's Theorem guarantees some $c$ in $(-1,1)$ with $f'(c)=0$. Here $f'(x)=2x$, so $c=0$.

## 🔗 Connection

Rolle's Theorem is the Mean Value Theorem when the secant slope is $0$.

## 🔑 Remember

Same endpoint height plus smoothness guarantees a horizontal tangent inside.
