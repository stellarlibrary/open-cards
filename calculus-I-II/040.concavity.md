# Concavity

## 🧠 Big Idea

Concavity describes the way a graph bends. A graph can be increasing and still bend downward, or decreasing and still bend upward.

The second derivative measures this bending because it tells how the first derivative is changing.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f''(x)>0 \Rightarrow \text{concave up}, \qquad f''(x)<0 \Rightarrow \text{concave down}
$$

Here $f''(x)$ is the derivative of $f'(x)$. If $f''(x)>0$, the slopes are increasing as you move right. If $f''(x)<0$, the slopes are decreasing as you move right.

## 👀 Intuition

Concave up looks like a cup that could hold water. The graph bends upward, and tangent slopes become larger. Concave down looks like a cap or an upside-down cup. The graph bends downward, and tangent slopes become smaller.

This is different from increasing or decreasing. Increasing tells whether outputs rise; concavity tells whether the rising or falling is speeding up or slowing down.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^3$. Then

$$
f''(x)=6x
$$

This second derivative is negative when $x<0$ and positive when $x>0$. So the graph is concave down on $(-\infty,0)$ and concave up on $(0,\infty)$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Concavity is set by the sign of $f''$, not $f'$. A function can be increasing ($f'>0$) yet concave down ($f''<0$) — like the early part of a square-root curve, which keeps rising but flattens as its slope shrinks.

## 🔗 Connection

Concavity helps explain the shape behind the Second Derivative Test and helps locate possible inflection points.

## 🔑 Remember

Concavity is about bending: $f''>0$ bends up, and $f''<0$ bends down.
