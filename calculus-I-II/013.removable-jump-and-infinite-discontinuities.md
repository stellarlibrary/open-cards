# Removable, Jump, and Infinite Discontinuities

## 🧠 Big Idea

Not all breaks in a function look the same. There are three main types of discontinuity, and each one fails continuity in its own way.

Knowing the type tells you what's happening to the graph: a hole, a step, or a blow-up.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

- **Removable:** $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists but $\neq f(a)$ (or $f(a)$ is missing)
- **Jump:** $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) \neq \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)$ (both finite)
- **Infinite:** $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)=\pm\infty$

In words: a removable break is a single hole, a jump is a sudden step between two finite levels, and an infinite break is a vertical asymptote.

## 👀 Intuition

- Removable: a tiny hole you could "fill in" with one point
- Jump: a staircase step where the graph leaps
- Infinite: the graph rockets up or down a vertical wall

## 🔍 Simple Example

$\dfrac{x^2-1}{x-1}$ has a **removable** hole at $x=1$, since the limit is $2$ but the point is missing.

The step function jumping from $0$ to $1$ at $x=0$ has a **jump**.

$\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ has an **infinite** discontinuity at $x=0$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Only a **removable** discontinuity can be "fixed" by redefining a single point, because the limit already exists there. Jump and infinite discontinuities can't be repaired this way — the limit either disagrees across sides or doesn't exist at all.

## 🔑 Remember

Hole, step, or wall: removable fills in, jump leaps, infinite shoots to a vertical asymptote.
