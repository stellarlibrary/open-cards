# Parametric Equations

## 🧠 Big Idea

Parametric equations describe a curve by letting a third variable, usually $t$, control both coordinates at once.

Instead of saying directly that $y$ depends on $x$, we say where the point is at each value of $t$. This is especially useful when a curve is traced over time, has direction, or fails the vertical line test.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
x=f(t), \qquad y=g(t)
$$

This means the horizontal coordinate is determined by $f(t)$, and the vertical coordinate is determined by $g(t)$. The parameter $t$ is not usually a coordinate on the graph; it is the input that tells the moving point where to be.

As $t$ changes, the point $(x,y)=(f(t),g(t))$ moves through the plane and traces a curve.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a bug crawling on a sheet of paper. At every second $t$, it has an $x$-position and a $y$-position. The path you see is the curve, but the parameter tells the story of how the bug moves along it.

Parametric equations keep this motion information. A regular equation may show the shape, but it often hides direction and timing.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let

$$
x=t+1, \qquad y=t^2
$$

This means each value of $t$ gives one point on the curve. For example, when $t=0$, the point is $(1,0)$; when $t=2$, the point is $(3,4)$.

As $t$ increases, the points trace the parabola-like path in a particular direction.

## 🔑 Remember

Parametric equations describe a curve by describing a moving point.
