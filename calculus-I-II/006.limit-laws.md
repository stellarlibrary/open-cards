# Limit Laws

## 🧠 Big Idea

Limit laws let you break a complicated limit into smaller, easier pieces. If you know the limits of the parts, you can combine them in natural ways.

The big message: limits respect basic arithmetic. Sums, products, and quotients of limits behave the way you'd hope.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

If $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x)$ both exist, then

$$
\lim_{x \to a} \big[f(x) \pm g(x)\big]=\lim_{x \to a} f(x) \pm \lim_{x \to a} g(x)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to a} \big[f(x)\,g(x)\big]=\lim_{x \to a} f(x)\cdot \lim_{x \to a} g(x)
$$

In words: the limit of a sum is the sum of the limits, and the limit of a product is the product of the limits. Quotients work the same way, as long as the bottom limit isn't zero.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of each piece of the expression heading toward its own destination. The whole expression simply combines those destinations using the same arithmetic.

## 🔍 Simple Example

$$
\lim_{x \to 2} (x^2+3x)=\lim_{x \to 2} x^2+\lim_{x \to 2} 3x=4+6=10
$$

Each part is evaluated separately, then added.

## ⚠️ Careful

The laws require each piece's limit to **exist**. You can't split a quotient when the denominator's limit is zero — that often signals an indeterminate form.

## 🔑 Remember

Limits split across sums, products, and quotients — as long as each piece exists.
