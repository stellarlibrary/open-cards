# Interval of Convergence

## 🧠 Big Idea

The interval of convergence is the complete set of $x$-values where a power series actually converges. It is more detailed than the radius of convergence because it includes endpoint decisions.

A power series centered at $a$ usually converges inside $|x-a|<R$ and diverges outside $|x-a|>R$. But at the boundary points $a-R$ and $a+R$, anything can happen.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
a-R < x < a+R
$$

This is the open interval determined by the center $a$ and radius $R$. It describes the values definitely inside the convergence region.

To get the full interval of convergence, test the two endpoints:

$$
x=a-R \quad \text{and} \quad x=a+R
$$

These are the boundary values where the ratio or root test often becomes inconclusive.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of the radius as telling you the size of a doorway, while the interval tells you exactly which points are allowed through. The interior is automatic once $R$ is known, but each endpoint has its own personality.

One endpoint might converge absolutely, the other conditionally, or neither might converge.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Consider

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}
$$

The radius is $R=1$, so the series converges for $-1<x<1$. At $x=1$, it becomes

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}
$$

This is the harmonic series, so it diverges. At $x=-1$, it becomes $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{n}$, which converges by the alternating series test. The interval is $[-1,1)$.

## 🔑 Remember

The interval of convergence is the radius plus the endpoint results.
