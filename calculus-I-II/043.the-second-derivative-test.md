# The Second Derivative Test

## 🧠 Big Idea

The Second Derivative Test uses concavity to classify a critical point. If the graph is bending upward at a horizontal tangent, the point is a local minimum. If it is bending downward, the point is a local maximum.

This test is quick, but it only works when the second derivative gives clear information.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f'(c)=0, \quad f''(c)>0 \Rightarrow \text{local minimum}, \qquad f''(c)<0 \Rightarrow \text{local maximum}
$$

The condition $f'(c)=0$ says the tangent is horizontal at $c$. If $f''(c)>0$, the graph is concave up there, so the point sits at the bottom of a cup. If $f''(c)<0$, the graph is concave down there, so the point sits at the top of a cap.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine placing a marble on the curve. In a concave-up bowl, the marble settles at the bottom: a local minimum. On a concave-down dome, the top is locally highest: a local maximum.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^3-3x$. Then $f'(x)=3x^2-3$, so the critical numbers are $x=-1$ and $x=1$. Also,

$$
f''(x)=6x
$$

Since $f''(-1)=-6$, there is a local maximum at $x=-1$. Since $f''(1)=6$, there is a local minimum at $x=1$.

## ⚠️ Careful

If $f''(c)=0$, the test is inconclusive. The point could be a maximum, a minimum, or neither.

## 🔑 Remember

At a horizontal tangent, concave up means minimum and concave down means maximum.
