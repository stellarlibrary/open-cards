# Related Rates

## 🧠 Big Idea

When two quantities are linked by an equation, their **rates of change** are linked too. Related rates problems use one known rate to find another, with time as the hidden variable.

The key step is differentiating a relationship with respect to time $t$.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

If $x$ and $y$ are related and both change over time, differentiate the relationship with respect to $t$. For example, from $x^2+y^2=r^2$:

$$
2x\frac{dx}{dt}+2y\frac{dy}{dt}=0
$$

This means: each variable's rate $\frac{dx}{dt}$, $\frac{dy}{dt}$ enters through the chain rule. Plug in the known values and solve for the unknown rate.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a ladder sliding down a wall. As the base slides out, the top slides down — the two motions are tied by the ladder's fixed length. Knowing how fast the base moves lets you find how fast the top falls.

## 🔍 Simple Example

A circle's area is $A=\pi r^2$. If the radius grows at $\frac{dr}{dt}=2$ when $r=3$:

$$
\frac{dA}{dt}=2\pi r\frac{dr}{dt}=2\pi(3)(2)=12\pi
$$

## ⚠️ Careful

Differentiate **before** plugging in numbers. Substituting fixed values too early freezes the variables and gives a wrong (often zero) rate.

## 🔗 Connection

Related rates are powered by **implicit differentiation**, where the hidden variable is time $t$. A clear sketch and a labeled relationship between the quantities is usually the hardest and most important step.

## 🔑 Remember

Relate the quantities, differentiate with respect to time, then substitute and solve.
