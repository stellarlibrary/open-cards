# Slope Fields

## 🧠 Big Idea

A slope field is a picture of a differential equation. It shows the slope that a solution curve should have at many different points in the plane.

Instead of solving the equation first, a slope field helps you see the behavior of possible solutions visually. You can often tell whether solutions rise, fall, level off, or move toward a long-term value.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=F(x,y)
$$

This means the slope of a solution curve at the point $(x,y)$ is given by the rule $F(x,y)$. A slope field draws a short line segment at many points, and each segment has the slope prescribed by the differential equation.

The equation gives local slope information. The slope field organizes that local information into a global picture.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine wind arrows showing the direction a leaf would move at each location. A slope field works similarly, except the arrows are tiny tangent lines for solution curves.

A solution curve is like a path that follows the direction suggested by nearby segments. It should pass through the field smoothly, matching the local tilt as it goes.

## 🔍 Simple Example

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=x-y
$$

This equation says the slope at each point is found by subtracting the $y$-coordinate from the $x$-coordinate.

At $(1,0)$, the slope is $1-0=1$, so the segment tilts upward. At $(0,1)$, the slope is $0-1=-1$, so the segment tilts downward. At points where $x=y$, the slope is $0$, so the segments are horizontal.

These small slopes help sketch the shape of solutions without first finding exact formulas.

## 🔑 Remember

A slope field turns a differential equation into a picture of possible solution curves.
