# Sequences

## 🧠 Big Idea

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers. Instead of depending on a continuous input like $x$, a sequence depends on a counting number: first term, second term, third term, and so on.

Sequences let calculus study patterns that unfold step by step. The main question is not only “what is the next term?” but “what happens in the long run?”

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\{a_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=a_1,a_2,a_3,\dots
$$

This means the sequence has terms named $a_n$, where $n$ is a positive integer. The symbol $a_1$ means the first term, $a_2$ the second term, and $a_n$ the general term.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a sequence as snapshots taken at times $n=1,2,3,\dots$. Each snapshot gives one number. Unlike a graph of a function over all real $x$, a sequence lives only at separated input values.

That separated structure is useful. It lets us track repeated processes, approximations, populations by generation, or partial answers that get refined over time.

## 🔍 Simple Example

The formula $a_n=\frac{1}{n}$ gives the sequence $1,\frac12,\frac13,\frac14,\dots$. Each new term is positive but smaller than the last.

This example shows a sequence whose terms move closer and closer to $0$, even though no term actually equals $0$.

## 🔗 Connection

Infinite series are built from sequences: a series adds the terms of a sequence together.

## 🔑 Remember

A sequence is a function whose input is a counting number.
