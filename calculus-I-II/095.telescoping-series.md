# Telescoping Series

## 🧠 Big Idea

A telescoping series is designed so that most middle terms cancel. Instead of adding infinitely many complicated pieces directly, you rewrite the terms to expose cancellation.

The final value comes from what survives at the beginning and what remains at the far end. This makes telescoping feel less like summing forever and more like simplifying one long expression.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (b_n-b_{n+1})=b_1-\lim_{N\to\infty} b_{N+1}
$$

This means each term subtracts the next piece of a sequence $b_n$. In the partial sums, nearly everything cancels, leaving the first term $b_1$ and the limiting tail term.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a collapsible telescope. When it is extended, it has many visible sections. When it collapses, the middle sections slide inside each other, leaving only the ends.

A telescoping series works the same way: the long sum compresses into a few boundary terms.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Consider $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n(n+1)}$. Since $\frac{1}{n(n+1)}=\frac1n-\frac{1}{n+1}$, the partial sums become

$$
\left(1-\frac12\right)+\left(\frac12-\frac13\right)+\cdots+\left(\frac1N-\frac{1}{N+1}\right)=1-\frac{1}{N+1}
$$

This means all middle fractions cancel in pairs. As $N\to\infty$, the remaining tail $\frac{1}{N+1}$ goes to $0$, so the series sums to $1$.

## ⚠️ Careful

Telescoping requires checking the limit of the leftover tail. Cancellation alone does not finish the convergence question.

## 🔑 Remember

A telescoping series cancels from the middle and leaves only the ends.
