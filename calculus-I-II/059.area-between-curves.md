# Area Between Curves

## 🧠 Big Idea

Area between curves measures the size of the region trapped between two graphs.

Instead of measuring area from a curve down to the $x$-axis, we measure the vertical gap between an upper curve and a lower curve. A definite integral then adds up all those tiny gaps across the interval.

The main question is: at each $x$, how tall is the region?

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
A=\int_a^b \big(f(x)-g(x)\big)\,dx
$$

Here $A$ is the area of the region, $[a,b]$ is the interval in $x$, $f(x)$ is the top curve, and $g(x)$ is the bottom curve. The expression $f(x)-g(x)$ is the height of a thin vertical slice.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine cutting the region into many skinny vertical rectangles. Each rectangle has width $dx$ and height “top minus bottom.” The integral adds all those rectangle areas together.

This is why order matters: area is positive, so the height should be upper curve minus lower curve.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Find the area between $f(x)=x+2$ and $g(x)=x^2$ on $0\le x\le 1$.

On this interval, $x+2$ is above $x^2$, so the area is $\int_0^1 (x+2-x^2)\,dx=\frac{13}{6}$. That number represents the total space between the two graphs from $x=0$ to $x=1$.

## ⚠️ Careful

If the curves cross, the “top” and “bottom” may switch. Split the interval at crossing points before integrating.

## 🔑 Remember

Area between curves is the integral of top minus bottom.
