# Quotient Rule

## 🧠 Big Idea

The quotient rule differentiates a fraction of two functions. Like the product rule, it must account for both the top and bottom changing — but division makes the bottom's effect work against the top.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)'=\frac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}
$$

This means:

- $f'g$: the top changes while the bottom holds
- $fg'$: the bottom changes while the top holds (this term is **subtracted**)
- divide by $g^2$ to account for the denominator

The order matters: it's $f'g$ **minus** $fg'$, not the other way around.

## 👀 Intuition

Increasing the numerator makes the fraction larger, so $f'g$ adds. Increasing the denominator makes the fraction *smaller*, so $fg'$ subtracts. Dividing by $g^2$ scales the change correctly for a quotient.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Differentiate $h(x)=\dfrac{x}{x+1}$. Let $f=x$, $g=x+1$:

$$
h'(x)=\frac{(1)(x+1)-(x)(1)}{(x+1)^2}=\frac{x+1-x}{(x+1)^2}=\frac{1}{(x+1)^2}
$$

## ⚠️ Careful

The minus sign and order are easy to flip. Remember "**low d-high minus high d-low, over low squared**": denominator times derivative of numerator, minus the reverse.

## 🔗 Connection

A quotient $\frac{f}{g}$ is just the product $f \cdot g^{-1}$, so you can also use the product and chain rules instead. The quotient rule is simply that combination packaged into one tidy formula, which is handy when rewriting as a product would be messy.

## 🔑 Remember

Quotient rule: $(f'g-fg')/g^2$ — top-derivative first, then subtract, all over bottom squared.
