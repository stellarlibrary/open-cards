# Numerical Integration

## 🧠 Big Idea

Numerical integration estimates a definite integral when an exact antiderivative is hard, unavailable, or unnecessary. Instead of finding a perfect formula for area, we approximate the area using simple shapes.

The most common idea is to divide the interval into small pieces. On each piece, replace the curve with something easier, such as a rectangle, a trapezoid, or a parabola.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
T_n=\frac{\Delta x}{2}\left[f(x_0)+2f(x_1)+\cdots+2f(x_{n-1})+f(x_n)\right], \qquad \Delta x=\frac{b-a}{n}
$$

This is the trapezoidal rule. The interval $[a,b]$ is split into $n$ equal parts of width $\Delta x$. The points $x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_n$ are the endpoints. The first and last function values are used once, while the interior values are used twice because each belongs to two neighboring trapezoids.

## 👀 Intuition

A trapezoid has a slanted top, so it often follows a curve better than a flat rectangle. More subintervals usually mean thinner trapezoids, and thinner trapezoids usually mean a better estimate.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Estimate

$$
\int_0^2 x^2\,dx
$$

using the trapezoidal rule with $n=2$. Then $\Delta x=1$, and the points are $x_0=0$, $x_1=1$, and $x_2=2$. Since $f(x)=x^2$,

$$
T_2=\frac{1}{2}\left[f(0)+2f(1)+f(2)\right]=\frac12(0+2+4)=3
$$

So the estimated area is $3$. The exact value is $\frac83$, so this estimate is close but slightly high.

## 🔑 Remember

Numerical integration replaces a hard area with many small easy areas.
