# Increasing and Decreasing Functions

## 🧠 Big Idea

A function is increasing when its outputs rise as you move left to right. It is decreasing when its outputs fall as you move left to right.

Derivatives make this precise: the sign of $f'(x)$ tells whether the graph is moving upward or downward on an interval.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f'(x)>0 \Rightarrow f \text{ is increasing}, \qquad f'(x)<0 \Rightarrow f \text{ is decreasing}
$$

This means positive slope pushes the graph upward as $x$ increases, while negative slope pushes it downward. The statement is about an interval, not just one isolated point.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of driving on a road from left to right. If your car is climbing, the road has positive slope and the height increases. If your car is descending, the road has negative slope and the height decreases.

A derivative sign chart is like a road report for the graph: plus signs mean uphill, minus signs mean downhill.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^2$. Then

$$
f'(x)=2x
$$

This derivative is negative when $x<0$, zero when $x=0$, and positive when $x>0$. So $f$ decreases on $(-\infty,0)$ and increases on $(0,\infty)$.

## ⚠️ Careful

A single point where $f'(x)=0$ does not make a whole interval increasing or decreasing. Look at the sign of $f'(x)$ across intervals.

## 🔑 Remember

Positive derivative means rising; negative derivative means falling.
