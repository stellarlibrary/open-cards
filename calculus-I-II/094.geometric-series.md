# Geometric Series

## 🧠 Big Idea

A geometric series adds terms that change by the same constant ratio each step. Because the terms shrink by repeated multiplication, this is one of the few infinite series whose sum we can write exactly.

The behavior depends completely on the ratio $r$. If repeated multiplication by $r$ drives terms toward $0$ fast enough, the total can be finite.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} ar^n=\frac{a}{1-r}, \qquad |r|<1
$$

This says that if the first term is $a$ and each term is multiplied by $r$, then the infinite sum equals $\frac{a}{1-r}$, but only when $|r|<1$. The condition means the powers $r^n$ shrink toward $0$.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture covering a wall by painting half of what remains, then half of what remains again, and so on. Each new painted amount is a fixed fraction of the previous one.

Even though the process has infinitely many steps, the total painted area approaches the whole wall.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $1+\frac12+\frac14+\frac18+\cdots$, we have $a=1$ and $r=\frac12$.

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac12\right)^n=\frac{1}{1-\frac12}=2
$$

This means the running total gets closer and closer to $2$, even though no finite partial sum equals $2$ exactly.

## ⚠️ Careful

If $|r|\ge 1$, the geometric series diverges. The formula $\frac{a}{1-r}$ is not valid then.

## 🔑 Remember

A geometric series sums to $\frac{a}{1-r}$ only when $|r|<1$.
