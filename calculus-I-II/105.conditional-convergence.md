# Conditional Convergence

## 🧠 Big Idea

A series converges conditionally when the signed series converges, but the absolute value series diverges. Its convergence depends on cancellation between positive and negative terms.

Conditional convergence is real convergence, but it is more delicate than absolute convergence.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \text{ converges, but } \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_n| \text{ diverges}
$$

This means the original signs produce enough cancellation for a finite sum, while the total magnitude of the terms is still infinite.

## 👀 Intuition

Picture walking right, then left, then right, then left, with steps that shrink to zero. You may approach a final location because of the alternating directions.

But if the total distance walked is infinite, the convergence comes from cancellation, not from having only a finite amount of movement.

## 🔍 Simple Example

The alternating harmonic series is $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\frac1n$. It converges by the alternating series test because $\frac1n$ decreases to $0$.

Its absolute value series is

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|(-1)^{n+1}\frac1n\right|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1n
$$

This means the absolute value series is the harmonic series, which diverges. Therefore the alternating harmonic series converges conditionally.

## ⚠️ Careful

Do not call a series conditionally convergent until both facts are checked: the original series converges and the absolute value series diverges.

## 🔑 Remember

Conditional convergence means convergence by cancellation, not by finite total size.
