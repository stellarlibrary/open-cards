# Center of Mass

## 🧠 Big Idea

The center of mass is the balance point of an object or distribution of mass.

If mass is spread unevenly, the center of mass is pulled toward the heavier side. Integration finds this point by averaging position, but with each position weighted by how much mass is there.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

For a thin rod from $x=a$ to $x=b$ with density $\rho(x)$,

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{1}{m}\int_a^b x\rho(x)\,dx, \qquad m=\int_a^b \rho(x)\,dx
$$

Here $m$ is the total mass of the rod. The expression $x\rho(x)\,dx$ is a tiny contribution to moment: position times tiny mass. Dividing total moment by total mass gives $\bar{x}$, the balancing location on the $x$-axis.

## 👀 Intuition

Think of a seesaw with many small weights placed along it. A weight far from the origin has more turning effect than the same weight near the origin.

The center of mass is the point where all those turning effects balance. It is a weighted average, not usually the geometric middle.

## 🔍 Simple Example

A rod lies on $0\le x\le 2$ with density $\rho(x)=x+1$. Its mass is

$$
m=\int_0^2 (x+1)\,dx=4
$$

This means the rod has total mass $4$. Its moment about the origin is

$$
\int_0^2 x(x+1)\,dx=\int_0^2 (x^2+x)\,dx=\frac{14}{3}
$$

This total moment measures how strongly the rod's mass pulls right of the origin. Dividing moment by mass gives

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{14/3}{4}=\frac{7}{6}
$$

The balance point is at $x=7/6$, slightly to the right of the midpoint because density increases with $x$.

## 🔗 Connection

For regions in the plane, the same idea uses moments about the axes: center of mass equals moment divided by mass.

## 🔑 Remember

Center of mass is the weighted average position of mass.
