# Critical Numbers

## 🧠 Big Idea

A critical number is an input where a function might have an important turning point. It is a place where the derivative is either zero or does not exist, while the function itself is defined.

Critical numbers do not automatically give maximums or minimums. They are **candidates** that deserve closer attention.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

$$
f'(c)=0 \quad \text{or} \quad f'(c) \text{ does not exist}
$$

Here $c$ is a number in the domain of $f$. The condition $f'(c)=0$ means the tangent line is horizontal. The condition that $f'(c)$ does not exist can mean a corner, cusp, vertical tangent, or other sharp behavior.

## 👀 Intuition

Imagine walking along a smooth path. A critical number is a place where your direction may stop changing upward or downward in the usual way. You might flatten out, turn around, hit a sharp corner, or simply keep going.

That is why critical numbers are like “checkpoints” on a graph, not final answers by themselves.

## 🔍 Simple Example

Let $f(x)=x^3-3x$. Then

$$
f'(x)=3x^2-3=3(x-1)(x+1)
$$

This equation says the slope is zero when $x=1$ or $x=-1$. Since the derivative exists everywhere for this polynomial, the only critical numbers are $-1$ and $1$.

## ⚠️ Careful

A critical number must be in the domain of the original function. If $f$ is not defined at $c$, then $c$ is not a critical number of $f$.

## 🔑 Remember

Critical numbers are places where the derivative says, “look here more closely.”
