# The Tangent Line Interpretation

## 🧠 Big Idea

The derivative at a point equals the **slope of the tangent line** there — the straight line that just touches the curve and matches its direction at that instant.

This is the geometric meaning of a derivative: it tells you which way the curve is tilted at a single point.

## 📐 Equation Meaning

The tangent line at $x=a$ has slope $f'(a)$:

$$
y-f(a)=f'(a)\,(x-a)
$$

This means: the line passes through the point $(a, f(a))$ and rises with slope $f'(a)$. It's the best straight-line approximation of the curve near that point.

## 👀 Intuition

Start with a **secant** line through two points on the curve. As the second point slides toward the first, the secant pivots until it just grazes the curve. That limiting line is the tangent, and its slope is the derivative.

## 🔍 Simple Example

For $f(x)=x^2$, we have $f'(x)=2x$. At $x=1$:

- point: $(1, 1)$
- slope: $f'(1)=2$

So the tangent line is

$$
y-1=2(x-1) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=2x-1
$$

## ⚠️ Careful

A tangent line touches and matches direction, but it can still cross the curve elsewhere. "Tangent" means matching slope at that point, not touching only once.

## 🔑 Remember

The derivative is the slope of the tangent line — the curve's tilt at a single point.
