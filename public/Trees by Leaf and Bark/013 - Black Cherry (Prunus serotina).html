Black Cherry Prunus serotina

Field marks

The leaf has many small teeth along its narrow oval blade. The older bark breaks into overlapping dark plates with curling edges, resembling burnt chips.

Eastern North America • Woodland gaps and edges. Young trunks look quite different from the mature bark shown here.

Identification note

On saplings and young limbs, the bark is smooth and dark reddish-brown with prominent horizontal pale lenticels that can recall a dark birch before breaking into mature plates. Turning a mature leaf over often reveals a fringe of rusty-brown fuzz along the lower midrib that Chokecherry lacks.
