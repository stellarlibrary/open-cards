White Oak Quercus alba

Field marks

The leaf lobes end in smooth rounded tips without bristles. The gray bark develops small rough scales or flaky plates rather than peeling in thin paper sheets.

Eastern North America • Deciduous woodland. Compare the leaf lobe tips with those of a red oak.

Identification note

Members of the red oak group (Quercus rubra and allies) end each leaf lobe in a fine hair-like bristle and keep darker, tighter bark with flat ridges. Even on one White Oak, sunlit upper leaves often have deeper sinuses than shaded leaves, so rely on the smooth lobe tips.
