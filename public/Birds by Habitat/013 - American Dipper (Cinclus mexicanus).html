American Dipper Cinclus mexicanus

Field marks

A plump slate-gray bird with a short, often cocked tail. The plain plumage and squat shape at fast water are better clues than expecting a long-legged wader.

Western North America into Central America • Fast, rocky streams. The bird often pauses on a rock beside moving water.

Identification note

North America's only truly aquatic songbird, it bobs up and down on stream rocks, flashes white-feathered eyelids when blinking, and dives underwater for insect larvae. Juveniles look paler below with a yellowish bill base, while the Eurasian White-throated Dipper has a crisp white bib.
