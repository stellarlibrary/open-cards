Common Loon Gavia immer

Field marks

A striped white patch breaks up the dark neck, and a checkerboard of white marks covers the dark back. Together they are useful on a swimming adult in breeding plumage.

North America and the North Atlantic • Breeds on freshwater lakes; many move to coastal water outside the breeding season. The strong black-and-white contrast is characteristic of the breeding adult.

Identification note

In winter and juvenile plumages, plain gray-brown upperparts and a white throat with a pale indentation on the side of the neck replace the checkered breeding pattern. Unlike a swimming cormorant, a loon holds its heavy, dagger-shaped bill level rather than tilted upward.
