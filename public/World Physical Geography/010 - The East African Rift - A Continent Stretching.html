The East African Rift: A Continent Stretching

Running south from the Afar region (near 12°N, 42°E) toward the African Great Lakes and Mozambique, the East African Rift divides into eastern and western branches; deep Lake Tanganyika (near 6°S, 29.6°E) occupies the western rift.

Read the landscape

Long, deep lakes and frequent earthquakes occur together because diverging continental crust stretches and fractures along normal faults: down-dropped crustal blocks (grabens) collect water into narrow basins while ongoing fault slip releases seismic energy.
