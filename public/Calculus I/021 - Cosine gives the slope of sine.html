Cosine gives the slope of sine

For angles measured in radians, d(sin x)/dx=cos x at every real input. The sign of the cosine tells whether the sine curve is rising or falling locally when that slope is nonzero.

Worked example

On y=sin x at x=π, the height is sin π=0 and the tangent slope is cos π=−1. For a small positive h, sin(π−h)=sin h>0, while sin(π+h)=−sin h<0.

The curve therefore crosses the axis from above to below, in agreement with its negative slope.
